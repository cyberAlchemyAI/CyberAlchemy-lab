01 / The company brain

Keep the knowledge
behind the work.

A project carries more than tasks. It carries the meaning of its goals, the reasoning behind its decisions and the evidence of what happened.

The Lab gives that knowledge a continuing structure. Definitions state what important terms mean. Connected records show what was attempted, which evidence supports a claim and which decisions govern a change.

When work changes hands, the next person or agent has a place to recover those connections.

02 / How it works

From an intention
to an accountable next step.

  1. 01

    State what the work means.

    Express the intention and define the terms that determine success. “Complete” needs a criterion that everyone can examine.

  2. 02

    Connect the work to its evidence.

    Identify the sources, methods and observations behind an assessment. Keep a finding connected to what supports it.

  3. 03

    Make change explicit.

    A proposal, an accepted change and a deployment have different effects. Each follows its declared rules and authority.

  4. 04

    Continue from usable context.

    Give the next session the relevant project knowledge. Check that its context and permissions still apply before continuing.

03 / Working with agents

The project remembers.
The agent continues.

An agent session is temporary. The knowledge needed to continue the work belongs to the project.

The Lab organizes that durable knowledge as project state. Context views select the information needed for a task; ontology views show relevant concepts and their declared connections. A new session recovers its context from those views.

Short-term memory belongs to the session. If a view expires or permission no longer applies, the agent refreshes its context or pauses. Persisting a lesson also creates something to examine: later work needs evidence of its use and benefit.
