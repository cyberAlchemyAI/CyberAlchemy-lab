1. Create the registration form.

  1. Sign in to the organizer’s Google account, open Google Forms, and create a blank form.
  2. Name it CyberAlchemy Lab × Craft Hackathon — Registration. Describe the solo format, two categories, one-project/one-category requirement, revised dates, and one US$200 prize per category.
  3. Add required questions: display name (short answer); a public profile link (optional); confirmation that the entrant will build one individual project; acceptance of the final rules; and acknowledgment that the submitted project’s public materials may appear in the gallery and event promotion. Add a required checkbox confirming the participant is 18 or older.
  4. In the response settings, collect the signed-in account’s verified email, limit the form to one response, and allow response editing until registration closes. Do not ask for a separately typed email as the voting identity.
  5. Keep the response summary hidden. Connect responses to a new private spreadsheet using the Sheets control in the responses panel.
  6. Publish with responder access for anyone with the link. The one-response setting still requires Google sign-in. Copy the responder URL and use it as registration_url in website/hackathon.json.
  7. Keep registrations unavailable until October 16 at 18:00 Brasília time, then publish or enable responses. Set the close date/time to October 23 at 23:59 Brasília time.

Google documents sign-in and the one-response limit in form publishing and access, and account email collection in response management.

2. Create the project-submission form.

  1. Create another blank form called CyberAlchemy Lab × Craft Hackathon — Project Submission.
  2. Use the project questions below. Prefer repository and video URLs over file-upload questions.
  3. Collect verified account emails, permit one response per account, and allow edits until the submission deadline. Keep response summaries hidden and link to a separate private response sheet.
  4. Restrict responder access to registered participants as you approve their registrations. Check that the submitter used the same account as registration.
  5. Copy the responder URL to submission_url. Close submissions November 23 at 23:59 Brasília time.

3. Create the voting form after submissions close.

  1. Create CyberAlchemy Lab × Craft Hackathon — Participant Vote.
  2. Add two required multiple-choice questions, one for Studio front-end and one for Open projects. List only that category’s eligible project IDs/titles and an Abstain option. Do not use checkboxes or ratings; if a category has no eligible project, offer only Abstain.
  3. Add the project-gallery link to the form description and state: one final ballot, one vote per category or abstention, no self-voting, no ballot changes.
  4. Collect verified account emails and enable the one-response limit. Disable response editing and the result-summary view. Link responses to a private voting spreadsheet.
  5. At publication, manage responder access: allow only the registered Google Accounts on your reviewed final roster. Keep general access restricted. Set their role to responder; do not give editor access. Do not make this ballot accessible to anyone with its link.
  6. Copy the responder URL to voting_url. Open voting only after the final gallery and roster are ready. Open November 24 at 12:00 and close November 25 at 18:00 Brasília time.

The key control is the combination: a reviewed participant roster, restricted responder access, verified account emails, and one response per account. Sign-in alone does not prevent a person from registering multiple accounts.

These controls are described in Google’s access documentation. Google’s response-management documentation describes email collection, Sheets export, and scheduled closure.

4. Check the results before announcing winners.

Keep the original ballot export. Work in a separate audit sheet. For every ballot, check that the account is on the roster, it arrived on time, the chosen project is eligible, and the voter is not its creator. Exclude invalid self-votes; do not reopen voting for replacement ballots.

Validate each category choice independently; an invalid or self-vote in one category does not erase a valid other-category choice. Count by category and project ID. The most-voted eligible project in each category wins its US$200 prize, provided it receives at least one valid vote. Use a recorded draw for a tied category winner; unawarded prizes do not transfer. Publish aggregate project totals and the reasons for excluded ballots, while keeping voter identities and choices private.

5. Test with two accounts.

  • An approved participant account can open the ballot; an unlisted account cannot.
  • After a test ballot is submitted, a second response and a ballot edit are blocked.
  • The ballot allows one project choice or Abstain per category and never exposes running totals.
  • The recorded account identity matches registration, and a test self-vote is excluded in the audit.
  • Scheduled closure uses the intended time zone. Demo links work for ordinary participants.

Use test copies while configuring the controls. For the real event, use a fresh ballot with no test responses and the same verified settings.

6. Connect the forms to the website.

Edit website/hackathon.json, replacing each relevant null with its actual HTTPS responder URL. Add the organizer’s email to contact_email. Keep the date fields as configured unless the announced schedule changes. Save, then rebuild from the repository root:
