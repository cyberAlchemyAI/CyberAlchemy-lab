A lead has a valid business email and has requested a demonstration. In this illustration, Q1 has been accepted for this campaign-reporting comparison.

R1 · Completed comparison under Q1. The report points to its selected criterion, reporting period, source rows, and assessment method.

Sources and standing

These are fictional records used to explain the model. US$1,200 ÷ 120 = US$10 per recorded lead; US$1,200 ÷ 60 = US$20. Under Q1, US$1,200 ÷ 12 = US$100 per qualified lead; US$1,200 ÷ 15 = US$80.

The source rows, period, exact criterion version, owner, and acceptance decision belong with a real assessment. Neither comparison establishes revenue causation or gives anyone permission to change the budget.

Now use the team's selected qualification criterion. A has 12 qualified leads and B has 15. The comparison becomes US$100 against US$80 per qualified lead.

We have learned something about these records under this criterion. To decide what to do next, we still need to examine the sources, the reporting period, and what “qualified” is supposed to tell us.

And when experience challenges that meaning, the meaning needs its own route for change.

What if the company could show its working?

An answer becomes more useful when someone can recover its basis. Which meaning applies? Where did the information come from? What has been accepted, what is still proposed, and who may take the next step?

The Lab gives those questions identifiable subjects: definitions, sources, work, artifacts, decisions, and the connections between them. People and AI can return to the same recorded basis while keeping their different responsibilities.

Explore the company brain

Even the rules need a way to grow.

A company develops new work, new knowledge, and new ways of understanding both. Its first set of rules cannot anticipate everything it will encounter.

The Lab makes those governing meanings part of what can be examined. A definition has a scope and an accountable route to acceptance. An artifact keeps the version it depends on. Experience can challenge a method, and a reviewed successor can give later work a better basis.
