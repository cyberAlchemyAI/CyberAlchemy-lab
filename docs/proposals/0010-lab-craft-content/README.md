# Lab and Craft: UX, UI, and copy package

Date: **16 October 2026**  
Standing: **proposed design and copy for review**  
Delivery: **documents only**.

The Lab's purpose is to provide the conditions for systems to keep developing what they know, what they can do, and how they change. The open company brain makes that environment approachable through everyday work. Craft keeps its own proposition: **your new software factory, straight from the future**.

This package turns the reviewed content proposal into a whole-site UX/UI specification and a page-ready copy deck. It preserves English, the site's natural editorial voice, the paper-and-crimson identity, monospace typography, and the distinction between an illustrative story and an implemented capability. It does not build a preview or alter the current website.

## Read the package

| Document | Its job |
| --- | --- |
| [Content proposal](PROPOSAL.md) | Vision, product distinctions, examples, research, and the decisions recovered from the session |
| [UX/UI design](UX-UI-DESIGN.md) | Journeys, page composition, design-system rules, responsive layouts, component behavior, accessibility, and implementation criteria |
| [Copy deck](COPY-DECK.md) | Proposed page copy, guide content, creative headlines, labels, and interaction messages |
| [Content and route map](CONTENT-MAP.md) | The connection between page copy, destinations, maintained source owners, and existing material that must be preserved |
| [Review record](DESIGN-REVIEW.md) | Independent review, repairs, document checks, and what still needs implementation or reader testing |

The documents have separate responsibilities. The proposal owns the editorial intent and recorded decisions. The UX/UI specification owns proposed composition and behavior. The copy deck owns proposed public wording. The route map owns candidate destination assignments. Formal meanings and implementation evidence continue to belong to their existing repository sources.

## The experience in one pass

A reader recognizes their work, follows one example, and discovers the environment underneath it. The campaign story is the proposed common thread: an agreed criterion supports a comparison, experience challenges that criterion, and an examined successor changes the basis of later work. Earlier reports keep their original meaning. Authority, provenance, and lifecycle are questions about those subjects, rather than stages in a single universal process.

The reader can then explore another department, inspect the precise contracts, continue into research and operational material, or enter Craft's software-construction path. Technical readers retain direct lookup and existing URLs.

## What is settled

- Preserve the approved paper-and-crimson visual identity and natural conversational explanation.
- Keep the open company brain central and connected to practical work across domains.
- Explain how definitions acquire scoped authority and evolve, and how artifact contracts and the governance lenses shape the environment for continued development.
- Keep Craft's construction-and-evolution ambition larger than its code-review consequence.
- Retain unique content, exact formal statements, code, quotations, examples' conditions, and meaningful existing links.
- Deliver design and copy documents only; no preview, website build, runtime operation, or publication.

## What remains proposed

The exact public wording, the A/B/C entrance selection, new guide routes, component refinements, and page compositions are reviewable design choices. Candidate routes in the map do not exist merely because the document names them. The specification recommends one cohesive treatment for later implementation; it does not record user approval of a final information architecture.

Later implementation will need responsive browser checks, keyboard and assistive-technology checks, source/body preservation, search and route verification, and reader comprehension exercises. This package records those criteria without claiming they have already passed.
