## 1. One vision, several ways in

**The Lab's ultimate purpose is to provide the conditions for systems to keep growing and evolving.** A system needs to develop its understanding and capabilities, and sometimes change the meanings, methods, and rules on which its work depends. The existing model gives those changes identifiable subjects, accountable owners, evidence, and explicit routes.

The core proposition:

> **The Lab gives systems a way to develop what they know, what they can do, and how they are allowed to change.**

Definitions can earn authority for an exact scope. Artifact contracts make those meanings usable in recorded work. Provenance keeps the basis inspectable. Lifecycle specifies the conditions for change. Those governing structures can themselves acquire reviewed successors. The environment for growth comes from their composition.

The public story has four connected levels:

| Level | What the reader should understand |
| --- | --- |
| Larger purpose | Systems can keep developing beyond their initial design; companies, research collaborations, and agent teams are practical settings for that ambition |
| Organizational expression | The **open company brain** connects shared meanings, sources, purpose, work, evidence, decisions, and assessed learning |
| Software specialization | **Craft**, the software factory from the future, makes selected software models consequential in construction and evolution |
| Everyday entrance | A campaign, reply, handoff, investigation, or software change lets the reader see the underlying environment at work |

Use one common explanatory thread: a campaign comparison reveals why an agreed meaning matters; later evidence challenges that meaning; a reviewed successor changes the basis of later work while earlier reports retain their original bindings. Different reading paths can lead into that same story.

The user-confirmed direction is the vision, open company brain, practical breadth, creative voice, and Craft's larger factory proposition. The exact copy and information architecture remain proposed. Keep the three entrance alternatives in section 8 available for comparison.

“Indefinitely” expresses the ambition of continuing development without a chosen final form. It is not demonstrated infinite scale, perpetual improvement, or universal suitability. Growth can include correction and simplification; more rules, records, or output alone do not establish it. Existing mechanisms and scoped tests are distinguished in section 11.

## 2. The public story and its voice

### 2.1 The Lab: an open company brain

Proposed hero:

> **Your company knows more than anyone in it.**
>
> CyberAlchemy Lab is an open-source company brain for people and AI. Connect what your company knows to the work it does—and give that understanding a way to grow.

Proposed opening immediately below it:

> Agree on what a result means. See where an answer came from. Discover why a decision applies. When experience challenges the method, examine a better one and make the change explicit.
>
> Start with a customer reply, a campaign question, or a promise moving between teams. Carry the work forward and improve the basis on which it happens.

Primary action: **Explore a practical example**. Secondary actions: **Find your team's guide** and **Explore the source on GitHub**. Keep a compact implementation-status link near the examples. The explicit open-source descriptor and GitHub header/action remain visible.

The company brain includes shared vocabulary, intentions, products and projects, sources, methods, work, decisions, and experience. A restored agent session is one use of that wider structure. People and agents participate in it; durable project knowledge belongs to Project State.

“Open” means the project is open source and the model makes the basis of knowledge and action inspectable within applicable access. Company data need not be public. The metaphor does not grant autonomy, policy standing, or permission to act.

### 2.2 Make the future tangible

Use creative headlines to open a concrete subject. Give the following sentence the job of explaining it. Vary the rhythm instead of making every page another version of “Build X without Y.” Keep navigation literal even where article headings have personality.

Proposed supporting copy:

> **Get the know-how out of everyone's heads.**
>
> Someone knows how to handle the exception, which promise was made, or why that rule exists. Start with a real situation. Make the information, judgments, exceptions, and expected result available to the next person or agent.

> **Even the rules need a way to grow.**
>
> Agree on a meaning. Give it an owner, a scope, and a way to be challenged. When experience calls it into question, examine a successor and make the change explicit. Keep an account of what changed and why.

> **What it becomes should be bigger than what we first imagined.**
>
> Start with one method, one project, one useful piece of work. Give it a place to retain knowledge, test an idea, and revise how it operates. The ambition reaches beyond the first application.

“Bigger” describes the horizon of possibility, not a requirement for accumulating complexity. “Living company brain” can be used as a metaphor for participation, correction, and renewal; its concrete mechanisms should appear in the story rather than be implied by personification.

Its larger proposition is software construction and evolution; removing routine review of constructed code is one intended consequence of an assured construction flow.

## 4. The common thread: a campaign changes how the team thinks

### 4.1 The dashboard is smiling. Should you be?

**Fictional teaching example.** All numbers, criterion labels, people, reports, and proposed changes in this section are illustrative. The cards explain relationships; they are not valid Lab carriers or reports from an executed workflow.

Two campaigns each spent US$1,200 in the same selected period. A recorded 120 leads; B recorded 60. At first glance, A looks like the better buy. The team also has an agreed qualification criterion for this reporting purpose. Applying it gives 12 qualified leads for A and 15 for B.

| Observation under the selected criterion | Campaign A | Campaign B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spend | US$1,200 | US$1,200 |
| Recorded leads | 120 | 60 |
| Qualified leads | 12 | 15 |
| Cost per recorded lead | US$10 | US$20 |
| Cost per qualified lead | US$100 | US$80 |

The apparent winner changes with the question. Show the selected source rows, observation window, arithmetic, and qualification meaning. Neither comparison alone establishes causal superiority or incremental revenue. A budget change needs its own decision.

### 4.2 Give the conclusion something to stand on

The team needs to know what “qualified” means, who accepted that meaning for this comparison, and which records were assessed against it. Introduce the governance vocabulary through those questions:

| Reader question | Model lens | What to inspect in this case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which meaning applies, and who can change it? | Authority | The exact selected definition, purpose, owner, scope, and decision |
| Where did this result come from? | Provenance | The source rows, reporting window, method, transformations, and responsible recorder |
| What state is each subject in, and what can happen next? | Lifecycle | The applicable state and transition conditions for the definition, report, proposal, or decision separately |

A report connects these subjects; it does not collapse their standing. Accepting a definition does not confirm every reported observation or authorize spending.

### 4.3 Even the rules need a way to grow

Make the proposed successor concrete:

| Fictional criterion | Meaning for the declared campaign-reporting scope | Example consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | A lead has a valid business email and has requested a demonstration | Record L42 satisfies those two conditions and qualifies under Q1 |
| Q2, proposed successor | Retain Q1's conditions and require a recorded assessment that the organization fits the selected target segment | L42 has no such assessment and cannot yet be classified as qualified under Q2 |

Later observations suggest the existing criterion misses something important about progression. Keep those observations and competing explanations. A proposed Q2 needs a clear scope, exclusions, evidence, affected consumers, and the relevant owner review. The evidence may support a revision, a different hypothesis, or no change. It does not alter Q1 by itself.

The report's record contract identifies the exact criterion it uses and the other required fields. Any necessary type-contract change follows its own route; a changed criterion does not automatically mean a schema change. Owner acceptance, adoption for later reporting, record conformance, and permission to change a campaign remain separately identifiable.

### 4.4 The old report keeps its history

Illustrative report cards:

| Card | Bound meaning and basis | What the reader should conclude |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 · completed comparison | Q1, selected period, source rows, and comparison method; counts 12 and 15 | The earlier result is still interpreted under Q1 |
| R2 · proposed later assessment | Q2 candidate and declared inputs; counts pending assessment | A proposed successor supplies no completed result or accepted use by itself |
| R2 · possible later completed assessment | Exact Q2 version after its required acceptance and adoption, new assessment record, selected inputs and observed counts | New results must be calculated and recorded under Q2; they do not overwrite R1 |

If the team later re-examines the earlier period under Q2, that is a separately identified assessment with its own bindings. It preserves the difference between “what we concluded then” and “what a different criterion now yields.” Later application and outcome evidence are needed to assess whether the change helped.

**The organization has a route for changing how it understands its work, while retaining an account of why.** A governed successor to a meaning can change the basis of future judgments. Section 6 explains the precise contracts; section 11 distinguishes those contracts from demonstrated execution.

### 4.5 What the reader can inspect

Show a small source extract, Q1's criterion card, R1, the observations challenging Q1, the Q2 proposal, and the separately recorded decision/adoption if included in the illustration. The reading path is **comparison → basis → challenge → successor → later use**. It is a narrative, not one mandatory lifecycle for every subject.

Use a compact inline view with optional expansion. The first view should make it clear that R1 still points to Q1 while a later assessment can use Q2 through its applicable route. Keep authority, provenance, and lifecycle as parallel questions on the named objects.

## 5. Other stories, different parts of the same environment

The campaign is the proposed explanatory spine. These companion guides show other responsibilities and preserve the maintained technical anchors. Each has one main job; link to section 6 for shared mechanisms rather than repeating the entire model.

### 5.1 A reply with a recoverable basis

Proposed headline: **The customer has already told your company this story.**

Fictional setup: a customer asks about a delayed refund. The selected material contains an accepted refund request, the applicable processing policy, an earlier promise of an update, and a payment export with no matching entry in its stated period.

1. **Recover the context.** Identify the exact request, customer correspondence, selected policy revision, export period, and relevant permissions. Show which identity matches are supplied by the fixture rather than claiming automatic resolution.
2. **Describe what is known.** The refund was accepted; a prior update was promised; no matching payment appears in the selected export. An absence in that file alone does not establish that no payment occurred elsewhere.
3. **Prepare useful work.** Produce a reply acknowledging the prior promise, explain the bounded observation, and prepare a finance investigation request. Keep the uncertain payment state visible.
4. **Record the decision.** An authorized person reviews the reply and next task. Preparing the draft and sending it remain separately recorded actions.
5. **Assess later experience.** If someone proposes a lesson about checking payment status before promising dates, specify its scope and prediction. Record actual application and later outcome separately; do not create a blanket rule from one complaint.

Illustrative output card, not a valid Lab record or an actual customer message:

> **Draft reply · awaiting review**
>
> Your refund request was accepted, and our previous reply promised an update. We still need to confirm whether the payment has been issued, so we can't give you a confirmed payment date yet.
>
> **Internal basis:** accepted request, previous correspondence, policy revision, selected export and period. No matching entry appears in that export; this does not establish that no payment occurred elsewhere. **Open question:** whether a payment exists outside the examined records. **Proposed next task:** finance investigation, subject to its applicable assignment and permission.

This gives the company brain a recognizable job: connect the customer's history, the business method, bounded investigation, and accountable continuation.

### 5.2 A handoff with its conditions intact

Proposed headline: **A promise shouldn't disappear at the handoff.**

Fictional setup: a sales conversation mentions a Friday delivery, an accepted proposal makes it conditional on receiving a customer dataset, and the implementation team has not yet received that dataset. The reader inspects each source rather than treating the most confident summary as authoritative.

Produce a handoff with the exact commitment, its condition, current evidence, responsible role, and unresolved next step. Keep “Friday was discussed,” “Friday was accepted unconditionally,” and “delivery is authorized and ready” as separate claims. Record any revised commitment through the relevant decision route.

Use a later software requirement to open the Craft branch: what must the feature do, which behavior must the selected construction preserve, and what checks support that claim? Link to the existing capacity demonstration for actual Craft scope. The fictional customer scenario does not establish that Craft implemented the promised feature.

The handoff emphasizes scope, authority, and current conditions. Reuse the campaign story for definition evolution and the existing technical anchors for their exact supported scope.

### 5.4 A lesson that should stay narrow

New fictional example: an agent observes a failed configuration change and proposes “always restart after changing configuration.” Other agents repeat it, including for changes supporting live reload.

Show the alternatives: retain the observation; leave the cause uncertain; describe the affected service/configuration class; test the proposed response; record interruptions and counterevidence; narrow, replace, or withdraw the advice as warranted.

Illustrative lesson excerpt, **not executable syntax or an admitted carrier**:

```text
Proposed advice: restart service S for configuration family C
Applies: this service version and configuration family
Excludes: live-reloadable configuration and other services
Prediction: the new configuration becomes active under the stated conditions
Counterevidence to seek: restart fails, causes avoidable interruption,
                       or the same result is obtained by live reload
Standing: candidate advice; no authority to restart a live service
```

Connect this example to the actual selector guards only where their supported inputs match. Do not fabricate a new general restart-governance implementation.

## 6. The environment underneath the stories

### 6.1 Governed meaning makes change examinable

Describing the Lab as memory plus learning leaves out its constitutive mechanism: how meanings come to govern, how they become usable contracts, and how the system changes those contracts without quietly changing what earlier work meant.

**Definitions establish a shared basis for judgment.** A Definition identifies a term, exact scope, owner, normative meaning, boundaries, and a route for challenge. Explanatory voices help people understand it while retaining their non-normative standing. A structurally complete or well-evidenced candidate still needs an exact owner promotion decision to become canonical semantic authority. Accepted meaning gives participants a criterion they can apply and dispute within scope; it does not establish empirical truth or grant permission to perform every action described by it. See [Definition](../../../docs/lab/reference/records/def-cal-definition-definition.md) and [Authority](../../../docs/lab/reference/records/def-cal-definition-authority.md).

**Artifact contracts make meaning consequential in recorded work.** An Artifact Type binds semantic Definitions to an exact representation contract, human projection, validation surface, ownership route, lifecycle posture, and claim ceiling. A particular artifact has its own identity, version, provenance, and standing. This lets people and tools examine the same declared subject through their respective views. Type admission, instance acceptance, observed execution, and authority remain separate. The governing [Artifact](../../../docs/lab/reference/records/def-cal-definition-artifact.md) definition also distinguishes canonical form and admitted process-origin declarations from governing authority. See [Artifact Type](../../../docs/lab/reference/records/def-cal-definition-artifact-type.md).

**Authority, provenance, and lifecycle shape each subject together.** They provide complementary questions rather than successive stages:

| Lens | Question | Contribution to an environment that can keep developing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authority | What may count for this purpose, under which exact meaning, owner, scope, and permitted effect? | Gives work a usable basis while keeping its boundaries and challenge route explicit |
| Provenance | Where did this version come from, through which sources, transformations, and responsible participants? | Lets later work inspect its inheritance, revisit an explanation, and preserve the limits of its support |
| Lifecycle | What is this subject's current state, and under what conditions may that state change? | Gives proposals, acceptance, use, replacement, or retirement their applicable routes rather than treating change as an overwrite |

The permitted states and transitions depend on the subject's actual contract; the examples above are not one mandatory lifecycle for every item. These lenses do not substitute for each other. A strong source trail does not confer authority. Acceptance does not improve the evidence. Passing one transition does not advance another subject's state. The [Authority Model](../../../docs/lab/reference/records/def-cal-definition-authority-model.md) and [Governance-Axis Independence Invariant](../../../docs/lab/reference/records/def-cal-definition-governance-axis-independence-invariant.md) make these distinctions explicit; [Provenance](../../../docs/lab/reference/records/def-cal-definition-provenance.md) preserves the origin and transformation account.

**Evolution includes the governing meanings.** A challenge or observation can motivate a proposed successor Definition, a revised type contract, or a different method. A changed normative meaning requires a new Definition version. A material change to an applicable artifact's normative meaning requires its owner-selected successor [Semantic Epoch](../../../docs/lab/reference/records/def-cal-definition-semantic-epoch.md); a changed encoding alone does not. Applicable owner review, admission, adoption, and effects retain their separate requirements. Earlier records keep their exact bindings, so a new criterion does not silently rewrite the basis on which an earlier result was assessed.

For cross-domain reuse, state the source contract and intended scope. The selected [Authority Promotion](../../../docs/lab/reference/records/def-cal-definition-authority-promotion.md) record describes an upstream CAV2 generalization route involving exercise, challenge, owner review, and separate domain adoption. Its source-specific ladder must not be presented as a universally executed Lab workflow. Its useful lesson here is that learning in one domain can inform another through an explicit route rather than transferring authority by analogy.

**The environment can develop its own means of development.** Definitions, type contracts, policies, profiles, and validation mechanisms are identifiable subjects of proposed change. Research and Improvement Targets can motivate revisions to them; an exact authorized [Admission Transaction](../../../docs/lab/reference/records/def-cal-definition-admission-transaction.md) applies the selected repository consequences. This gives the model a way to evolve how later evolution is described and assessed. It establishes neither automatic self-modification authority nor universal runtime enforcement.

Memory, learning, and individual applications become useful within that environment. The company brain is its organizational expression; Craft specializes the software-construction part. Demonstrate the composition through exact contracts and examples, with current implementation evidence alongside it.

### 6.4 Learning without a growing wall of instructions

Open with a familiar burden: a team has more checklists after each failure, yet repeats the same failures and spends more time satisfying procedure.

### 6.5 Agent societies and collective failure

## 7. Craft: the model stays on the factory floor

### 7.1 The software-factory problem

Follow the whole route: need, requirement, model, construction, evaluation, integration, delivery, maintenance, and later change. Identify clarification, coordination, waiting, omitted obligations, and rework where they arise.

Explain comprehension debt as the accumulated effort needed to recover the understanding required to explain, assess, or safely change a system. It can remain even when the code is readable: the lost material may be the requirement, responsibility, state transition, or reason for a boundary.

Do not claim that a faster coding stage necessarily speeds delivery. Distinguish structural problems Craft addresses from reviewer availability, infrastructure capacity, or organizational delays that require other interventions.

### 7.2 What makes the model consequential

Introduce the explanatory chain:

**Domain meaning → explicit requirements and contracts → responsibilities and workflows → selected profiles and realizations → constructed artifacts → conformance and behavioral evidence.**

This is the content organization for explaining Craft's direction and supported examples, not a blanket assertion that every possible application already runs through a complete pipeline.

Explain the different jobs with small examples:

- A Rule evaluates a Boolean condition.
- An Operation owns a declared state-changing action and its effects.

Keep the user's ambition explicit: **routine manual inspection of constructed code should become unnecessary within a sufficiently assured construction flow**.

### 7.5 Drift, tangled dependencies, and successive changes

Use several changes to the same example. Show the preserved obligation, changed model, selected construction, observed artifact, and check result at each step.

Relevant failures include copied business rules, responsibilities combined for convenience, bypassed boundaries, ignored decisions, missing branches, and a caller left unchanged. Show which mechanism covers which case. Generic type validity cannot establish all of these properties.

The current Craft article distinguishes:

1. Model validation: declarations and references fit their contracts.
2. Artifact conformance: observed output agrees with selected construction.
3. Behavioral evaluation: execution meets expectations under declared conditions.
4. Completion assessment: the required work, including integration obligations, was actually performed.

Retain those distinctions. A conformance diagnostic is evidence of detection, not proof that the underlying issue has already been repaired.

### 7.6 The supported capacity example

Preserve the existing requirement exactly: requested units must be **strictly less than** available units. A request for 4 from 5 is accepted; 5 from 5 is rejected. Classification leaves the supplied snapshot unchanged.

| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | 5 | Accept |
| 5 | 5 | Reject |

pub fn within_capacity(snapshot: &CapacitySnapshot) -> bool {
    snapshot.requested_units < snapshot.available_units
}

pub fn classify_capacity(snapshot: &CapacitySnapshot) -> CapacityDecision {
    if within_capacity(snapshot) {
        CapacityDecision::Accept
    } else {
        CapacityDecision::Reject
    }
}

### 13.1 Algorithm distinction to preserve

- **Epidemic/gossip dissemination:** how information spreads among participants or replicas.
- **Stigmergic coordination:** how participants coordinate through changes in a shared environment or artifact.

The Lab should not be described as implementing epidemic algorithms based on this session.
