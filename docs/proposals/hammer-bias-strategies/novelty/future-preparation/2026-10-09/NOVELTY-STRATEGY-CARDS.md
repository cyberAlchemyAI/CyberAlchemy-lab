# Ten mutations for investigating novelty in analysis

Prepare a strategy by specifying a change that could create a new next move. Keep its memorable sentence as a starting prompt, then require an explicit operation, question or experiment. These cards are future candidates for investigation. They add no scored calls or results to the thirteen-study run.

## The common card

For every strategy, record:

1. **Anchor:** the valued outcome, supplied observations and hard constraints.
2. **Mutation:** one dependency, causal arrow, carrier, sequence or interaction to change.
3. **Construction:** the new operation or question that the change makes possible.
4. **Rival:** the closest ordinary explanation or operation that might already do the same work.
5. **Probe:** a feasible observation on which the proposal and rival predict different results.
6. **Defeat:** the result that would make us abandon or revise this proposal.
7. **Residue:** discarded ideas, unresolved conflicts and resource assumptions, each with a source ID.

Use two lanes throughout. The outcome lane protects the anchor and records the burden on every actor. The invention lane applies the mutation and keeps speculative openings explicit. An opening with an unknown indispensable resource remains a hypothesis; it needs a resource-acquisition step before a feasible-probe claim. Constraint changes need their own task variant and cannot count as solving the original problem.

## Strategy cards

| Candidate and starting sentence | Mutation and required creation | Investigation that could defeat it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Tool deprivation × two lanes.** “The hammer disappears. What must still hold?” | Remove the tool's actual operating dependency. The outcome lane preserves the joint's required function; a fresh invention role proposes another way to achieve it. | Compare actual dependency removal with banning only hammer-related words. Reject a proposal that restores the same dependency under a new name or drops the required outcome. |
| **2. Logical inversion × causal intervention.** “What if the hammer is teaching the world to grow nails?” | Reverse the presumed causal arrow into a hypothesis about tool use shaping later specifications or demand. Construct an intervention on that return path. | Hold initial demand and required outcome equal; compare return-link removal with a sham. If later dependence does not change as predicted, the proposed feedback account weakens. Reversing a sentence supplies no causal evidence. |
| **3. Destructive attack × fresh builder.** “Break the assumption; give the opening to someone else.” | An attacker breaks one named dependency and records what survives. A fresh builder receives the failure and proposes a replacement, requirement removal or separating experiment. | Compare with critique-only and an ordinary builder relay. Reject an alleged construction that quietly reinstalls the attacked dependency. An explicit no-opening is a useful negative result. |
| **4. Carrier migration × second attack.** “Let the joint inherit the hammer's job.” | Move the function from the preferred tool to the object, arrangement or environment. Attack the new carrier's key dependency next. | Account for displaced force, effort and actor burden. Compare the migrated operation with the original, including false signals and cancellation where relevant. Outsourcing the same work without an operational change fails this candidate. |
| **5. Robot Talks × delayed convergence.** “Let the disagreement design the experiment.” | Give investigators the same evidence and preserve two incompatible predictions until a fresh role proposes an action that separates them. | Compare immediate and delayed convergence using identical concern reports. The candidate fails if it generates extra debate without a distinct feasible probe, or the supposed rivals predict the same observation. |
| **6. Distill × residue-fed construction.** “The discarded footnote gets the next experiment.” | Distill produces both a decision summary and a source-bound residue record. A fresh builder receives the residue to construct an opening from what was omitted. | Compare summary-fed and residue-fed builders with the same base facts and allowances. Repetition, restored source content and new creation receive separate judgments. Fabricated residue fails. |
| **7. Protected anomaly × rival accounts.** “Give the awkward fact a vote on the next move.” | Protect an inconvenient supplied observation from premature dismissal. Generate two accounts that explain it through different dependencies. | Supply the same anomaly to the control. Change one condition and require opposing predictions. A more prominent retelling of the anomaly without a changed action fails. |
| **8. Rival backcasting × first divergence.** “Two successful futures enter; find where their paths split.” | Construct two plausible successful futures with different dependencies, then trace them back to the earliest present choice that distinguishes them. | Compare with one-future backcasting and ordinary reasoning. A first step that requires unavailable resources cannot earn feasible-probe credit; convergent paths provide no separating action. |
| **9. Mechanism collision × interface ablation.** “Keep both tools; interrupt their conversation.” | Connect two parent functions so that their interaction may create a new signal or operation. Then preserve both parents while breaking only the link. | Compare intact connection, mere co-presence, broken connection and each parent alone. If a parent already explains the result, the combination has not shown an added mechanism. Measure physical effects separately from proposal quality. |
| **10. Inverted objective × boundary search.** “At what point does more solution become more problem?” | Search for a condition where increasing the preferred intervention worsens the valued outcome. Construct a boundary detector or an operation that avoids that regime. | Compare intervention levels under fixed outcome and conditions, then test the proposed boundary on a fresh case. A boundary chosen after seeing results cannot support confirmation. |

Cards 2 and 10 target different arrows: card 2 concerns later demand created by using the tool; card 10 concerns conditions where the present intervention worsens the present outcome.

## One constructive attack, made concrete

Use the supplied observation “pressing one corner of a cover lifts the opposite corner.” The attack is: more closing pressure may itself produce opening through a pivoting relation. The constructive handoff is: use pressure location as a diagnostic variable. The fresh proposal is to move the pressure point while holding the cover and closure arrangement fixed, then record which corner lifts.

Predeclare a rival that predicts uniformly improved grip as pressure increases. A position-dependent reversal distinguishes the proposed pivoting account from that particular rival; no reversal weakens the opening. Keep other explanations available. This is a proposed experiment, not an observed mechanism or a demonstrated replacement closure.

The creative move is **turning the failure into a variable we can manipulate**. Its practical value depends on what the experiment reveals.

## Stage handoffs and a first comparison

The attacker passes a broken dependency and surviving outcome to the builder. Robot Talks passes incompatible predictions to the probe designer. Distill passes both the core and the omitted material forward with source IDs. A late editor may compress these artifacts only after the raw proposal has its independent assessment; new editor ideas need a separate assessment.

For the first successor comparison, use the existing [four-cell pilot](NOVELTY-PROCESS-PILOT.md): immediate versus delayed convergence, crossed with summary-fed versus residue-fed construction, plus ordinary and generic relays. Its prospective allowance is 88 native calls before separately declared overhead. No successor calls have run. Its [Dispatch Spec route](future-pilot.dispatch.json) has structural validation; execution readiness and effectiveness still need evidence.

Compare qualified new probe families, their distinct predictions, resource fit, uncertainty and actual burden. Preserve negative findings. Extra words, candidate counts and confident agreement are secondary observations and cannot by themselves establish useful novelty.
