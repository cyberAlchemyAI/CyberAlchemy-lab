# Twenty strategies for investigating how novelty emerges and can be learned

**Prepared:** 10 October 2026  
**Status:** Prepared research strategies, AC01–AC20; none of the new interventions has been experimentally executed.  
**Academic basis:** [26-reference synthesis](/home/vlad_/projects/CyberAlchemy-lab/docs/proposals/hammer-bias-strategies/novelty/academic-extension/2026-10-10/ACADEMIC-FOUNDATIONS.md) and [typed source register](/home/vlad_/projects/CyberAlchemy-lab/docs/proposals/hammer-bias-strategies/novelty/academic-extension/2026-10-10/SOURCES.json).  
**Continuity:** These cards extend the earlier hammer-bias research. They do not replace old IDs, regrade old studies or install default skill behavior.

## How to use the catalog

Select the bottleneck before selecting a card. State the required function without the favored tool; list hard constraints, actual materials, controls and resources; preserve ordinary and component repertoires; and declare the intended addition and defeat condition.

Each procedure below is a **project adaptation or research proposal**. Academic sources support the rationale identified in the companion evidence map. They do not validate the complete card or establish novelty to the academic field. Some cards deliberately refine existing techniques; their overlap is explicit.

“Lane A” and “Lane B” describe concerns or experimental roles. They do not establish canonical two-lane discipline compliance or authorize live delegation. Robot Talks can investigate tensions when its applicability conditions hold; Distill can carry a source-bound construction handoff. The prepared route references those capabilities without executing them.

For a future Deep exploration, a finite starting configuration is two concern tracks and at most three proposal/attack/construction rounds. Stop earlier if a round repeats the same structure under new names or lacks new evidence. This is a candidate preparation budget; it is not a trial performed here, a statistical sample size or a commitment to launch every card.

## Shared evidence contract

Every completed research attempt should preserve:

```text
Context and required function:
Hard constraints, preferences, conventions and unknowns:
Ordinary/component/upstream comparison repertoire:
Academic rationale and exact operative hypothesis:
Manipulated variable and information/cost controls:
Concrete changed operation or discriminating observation:
Attack: failure / invariant / available variable:
Selected summary or residue, declared before handoff:
Resources, burden and displaced work:
Feasibility, method application, local novelty and usefulness separately:
Defeat condition and no-opening boundary:
Retained state and unfamiliar-case test, if learning is claimed:
```

The phrase “fresh builder” describes an information boundary to investigate. If one investigator performs all passes, record that execution path; do not call it independent-agent evidence.

## Strategy cards

### AC01. Generic parts before customary use

**Bottleneck:** Function fixation: every candidate preserves the tool's customary action.

**Operative move:** Describe each available object or process as constituent parts with material, geometry, timing, signal and resource properties. Remove customary-use labels in a separate view. Generate a candidate from an underused property, then check it against the original facts.

**Procedure and handoff:** Lane A inventories properties without proposing uses; Lane B states the preserved function and prohibited scope changes. Exchange the inventories. The attacker identifies one property whose proposed use fails under a supported condition; the builder constructs from a surviving property. Distill retains the property, source fact and failure boundary.

**Required artifact:** A part/property table and one construction whose working property differs from the customary tool action.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** A hammer's head can be examined as mass and shape; its handle as a rigid member. These descriptions can generate a search for a weight, spacer or lever role, but available geometry and task facts must support the selected use.

**Comparison to prepare:** Whole-object descriptions versus generic parts, with the same objects, facts, output cap and construction time.

**Defeat condition:** No additional feasible mechanism after whole-object and upstream comparison; property reinterpretation depends on invented material behavior.

**Relationship to existing work:** Refines earlier affordance reuse and function migration by manipulating the description that precedes generation. It is an established technique adapted here, not a newly invented academic method.

**Academic rationale:** [A11][A11], [A12][A12]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC02. Extract a relation from two source analogies

**Bottleneck:** Analogy produces clever metaphors without a transferable operation.

**Operative move:** Compare two source mechanisms with different surface features and the same relevant relation. Extract a shared relational schema before mapping it to the target.

**Procedure and handoff:** Lane A identifies entities, relations and boundary conditions in each source; Lane B identifies target constraints. The attack names a relation that does not transfer. The builder must map each essential relation and list at least one source-target mismatch. Distill carries the mapping and mismatch, not just the analogy's name.

**Required artifact:** Two source maps, their shared schema, a target mapping and one executable candidate or precise no-transfer finding.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** If two verified mechanisms distribute a local load across multiple supports, map that relation to a joining problem only when the target actually supplies supports, transferable load paths and permissible changes.

**Comparison to prepare:** The same two source descriptions with ordinary analysis versus explicit comparison and mapping. Separately compare a single-source mapping if information differences can be controlled.

**Defeat condition:** The proposal borrows vocabulary but cannot map the operative relation; the needed target entity or resource is absent.

**Relationship to existing work:** Adds a two-source schema-induction requirement to the earlier analogy operator; tests relational transfer rather than metaphor diversity.

**Academic rationale:** [A10][A10]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC03. Change one rule of the solution grammar

**Bottleneck:** All alternatives remain inside a convention that excludes other mechanisms.

**Operative move:** Write the rules that make a candidate admissible. Separate hard task requirements from conventions. Modify exactly one confirmed convention and preserve the valued outcome.

**Procedure and handoff:** Lane A explores the existing grammar; Lane B proposes one rule change. The attack checks whether the changed rule was genuinely nonessential. A builder constructs a candidate that the original grammar could not express. Distill records both grammars and the exact outcome-preservation argument.

**Required artifact:** Old rule, changed rule, newly expressible operation and preserved constraints.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** A toy joining case may conventionally require every connection to use a discrete fastener. Relaxing that convention can admit a continuous interface only if the case's fixed material and service requirements permit it.

**Comparison to prepare:** Search in the original grammar versus search after the single declared change; compare equal-budget broader search in the original grammar.

**Defeat condition:** The candidate was already expressible, or its benefit comes from relaxing an essential requirement.

**Relationship to existing work:** Extends deprivation and representation change by exposing the generative rule itself. Transformation is a hypothesis about the search space, not proof of historical originality.

**Academic rationale:** [A03][A03], [A15][A15]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC04. Construct a provisional arrangement before interpreting it

**Bottleneck:** A fixed interpretation makes every combination an elaboration of the incumbent.

**Operative move:** Generate a small set of valid arrangements of known components before assigning each a target role; then explore their possible functions.

**Procedure and handoff:** Supply the same closed component set to both lanes. Lane A assembles bounded arrangements; Lane B assigns interpretations subject to the invariant. Swap roles once. Attack one interpretation and have a builder reconstruct a surviving arrangement. Distill preserves arrangement details needed for rebuilding.

**Required artifact:** Arrangement → interpretation → task-fit ledger, including rejected interpretations.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** In a declared toy inventory of a rigid strip, clamp and elastic element, generate permitted connection topologies before labeling one a fastening device. No new component or physical property can enter through interpretation.

**Comparison to prepare:** Function-first design versus provisional-arrangement-first design, with the same component set and number of candidates.

**Defeat condition:** Arrangement diversity never changes admissible operations, or interpretations invent capabilities not supplied by the inventory.

**Relationship to existing work:** A concrete generation/exploration variant of earlier recombination. The ordering intervention is new preparation for this program; the cognitive idea is established.

**Academic rationale:** [A09][A09], [A26][A26]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC05. Translate the same problem across representations

**Bottleneck:** A verbal description hides dependencies that are visible in another representation.

**Operative move:** Represent identical facts as a process graph, constraint table or time line, then reconstruct the verbal problem from that view.

**Procedure and handoff:** Lane A supplies the ordinary description; Lane B performs a loss-checked translation. The attack identifies an inconsistency between representations. A builder must exploit a newly visible relationship without introducing a new fact. Distill retains the relation plus the obligations that make the views equivalent.

**Required artifact:** Two representations, an equivalence check and one operation suggested by a newly visible relation.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** A joining problem described as a sequence of hammer actions can be represented as states: aligned, held, loaded, released. A removable holding operation becomes a candidate only if it preserves every required state transition.

**Comparison to prepare:** Ordinary analysis versus translation, using equal facts; use an additional-format control to separate representation effects from simply receiving more text.

**Defeat condition:** The diagram adds information, drops a constraint, or merely redraws the same action.

**Relationship to existing work:** Extends representation change with round-trip obligation checks and a changed-operation criterion.

**Academic rationale:** [A09][A09], [A16][A16]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC06. Let a failed construction expose the hidden problem frame

**Bottleneck:** Repeated construction failure indicates that the stated problem embeds an unsupported assumption.

**Operative move:** Use a bounded prototype or replay to discover which framing assumption caused failure. Revise the representation of the problem explicitly, preserving the required outcome.

**Procedure and handoff:** Lane A holds the original frame; Lane B proposes a candidate under it. The attacker links a specific failure to an assumption, not to a general dislike. The builder tries one revised frame. Record any scope delta; a changed outcome cannot be credited against the original task. Distill preserves the failure evidence and both frames.

**Required artifact:** Frame → candidate → observed or modeled failure → justified frame revision → new candidate.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** Failure of a permanent attachment can reveal that the case requires repeatable alignment and release. That fact must already be supported or obtained from the actual owner; it cannot be invented to make an alternative win.

**Comparison to prepare:** Fixed-frame iteration versus one evidence-bound reframing opportunity, with the same trial budget.

**Defeat condition:** The revision has no source-backed reason, or obtains improvement by weakening success criteria.

**Relationship to existing work:** Adds construction-driven problem/solution co-evolution to tool-independent framing. It is not permission to rewrite the user's goal.

**Academic rationale:** [A16][A16]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC07. Allocate separate budgets to breadth and depth

**Bottleneck:** Search either stays with one mechanism or produces many unfinished possibilities.

**Operative move:** Reserve part of a fixed budget for distinct mechanism categories and part for developing a candidate within a selected category.

**Procedure and handoff:** Lane A covers different categories without polishing; Lane B develops a predeclared subset. The attack checks both whether categories are truly different and whether development closes resource gaps. Distill carries one complete option and a bounded ledger of unresolved alternatives. Compare alternative allocations prospectively.

**Required artifact:** Mechanism-category map, within-category construction and spent-budget record.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** Search joining mechanisms by how they carry load, then develop one candidate's assembly, service and failure behavior. Four names for impact fastening count as one family if their causal operation is the same.

**Comparison to prepare:** Breadth-only, depth-only and a predeclared mixed allocation under equal total time or generation budget.

**Defeat condition:** Mixed allocation adds no qualified mechanism or worsens completion after cost is counted.

**Relationship to existing work:** Operationalizes flexibility and persistence as measured allocations. It sharpens two-lane design without equating these pathways to the canonical lanes.

**Academic rationale:** [A13][A13]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC08. Test incubation separately from fresh-context search

**Bottleneck:** A rehearsed interpretation persists despite repeated analysis.

**Operative move:** For humans, prepare the problem, introduce a declared interruption condition, then resume. For agents, separately compare continued context with a fresh context carrying a controlled fact pack.

**Procedure and handoff:** Use separate human and agent protocols. Keep total resources and cue exposure explicit. Human conditions can compare low-demand activity and uninterrupted work; agent conditions compare context/history availability without pretending elapsed waiting is cognition. Attack hidden extra information or budget. Distill preserves prerequisites and unresolved tensions before resumption.

**Required artifact:** Preparation/interruption/resumption record or context-intervention record, with new feasible mechanisms tracked.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** A person can return to the joining task after a matched interruption; an agent can receive the same essential facts without the incumbent's generated chain. Those are different interventions.

**Comparison to prepare:** Prepared interruption versus matched uninterrupted work for humans; continued versus fresh context for agents.

**Defeat condition:** No additional qualified candidate, unequal resource exposure, or a claim about incubation based only on delay between agent calls.

**Relationship to existing work:** Adds a moderator-aware timing study to delayed convergence; it does not repeat the earlier claim that postponing consensus must help.

**Academic rationale:** [A14][A14], [A12][A12]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC09. Generate before exposing solution examples

**Bottleneck:** A supplied example becomes an unnoticed template for every answer.

**Operative move:** Preserve a first candidate set before showing solution examples, then compare additions and feature copying after exposure.

**Procedure and handoff:** Lane A generates from task facts alone; Lane B separately records features of the examples. After the first set is saved, expose the same examples and permit revision. The attack asks whether a new feature is useful transfer or unexamined copying. Distill retains independent ideas as well as revisions.

**Required artifact:** Before/after candidate sets with example-feature provenance and mechanism-level differences.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** Save joining alternatives before showing a nail-based demonstration. After exposure, test whether alternatives acquire unnecessary impact or permanent-fastener features.

**Comparison to prepare:** Examples before generation, examples after preserved generation, and matched fact-only generation.

**Defeat condition:** Quarantine only removes useful knowledge, yields no repertoire gain, or creates infeasible options.

**Relationship to existing work:** Makes example timing and copying measurable inside earlier independence and fixation tactics. A no-examples policy is not assumed best.

**Academic rationale:** [A12][A12]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC10. Keep a familiar core and alter one unfamiliar connection

**Bottleneck:** Radical variation breaks too much at once to be understood or tested.

**Operative move:** Keep a verified subsystem and change one interface, relation or dependency. Identify the smallest unconventional insertion that could alter behavior.

**Procedure and handoff:** Lane A documents the core's closure; Lane B proposes the changed relation. The attack localizes the added uncertainty. A builder must account for the interface and preserve the core's obligations. Distill carries the stable boundary, changed link and one separating test.

**Required artifact:** Core, inserted relation, interface contract and localized test.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** Retain verified parts and change how their alignment information is transmitted or how one removable connection is made. Any unfamiliar element must have available supporting facts.

**Comparison to prepare:** Whole-system replacement versus single-link insertion, and ordinary improvement of the unchanged core.

**Defeat condition:** The insertion is already present upstream, adds no useful behavior, or hides incompatibility at the interface.

**Relationship to existing work:** Refines link migration into a deliberately bounded unconventional insertion. The bibliometric rationale is suggestive, not causal validation.

**Academic rationale:** [A21][A21], [A10][A10]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC11. Vary one soft constraint through a ladder

**Bottleneck:** The team treats all constraints as either absolute barriers or irrelevant limits.

**Operative move:** Choose one confirmed soft constraint and define a small ordered set of settings while holding the essential outcome and hard constraints fixed.

**Procedure and handoff:** Each lane constructs under a declared setting. The attack identifies which constraint caused a mechanism to appear or disappear. The builder tries the adjacent setting rather than changing several rules. Distill retains the activation threshold, burden and source status of the constraint.

**Required artifact:** Constraint setting → available mechanisms → usefulness and cost map.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** A toy joining case can vary a declared preference for assembly time while keeping load and service requirements fixed. A candidate that needs a relaxed hard requirement cannot enter the comparison.

**Comparison to prepare:** Predeclared settings of the same constraint with equal search budget; include the ordinary setting.

**Defeat condition:** No informative change in feasible mechanisms, or improved results require undeclared relaxation.

**Relationship to existing work:** Replaces generic deprivation with a typed, measurable constraint intervention. No monotonic benefit is assumed.

**Academic rationale:** [A15][A15]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC12. Require an attack to leave a constructive opening

**Bottleneck:** Critique produces refinements or objections but no additional mechanism.

**Operative move:** Every attack must identify a failed assumption, a preserved function and a permitted variable. A builder then constructs from that variable.

**Procedure and handoff:** Lane A defends one candidate; Lane B attacks a source-supported dependency. Save the same attack for both experimental conditions. In one condition a fresh builder receives critique only; in the other it also receives the explicit invariant/opening contract. Distill preserves the actual opening or terminal no-opening result.

**Required artifact:** Failure → invariant → variable → construction → defeat chain, with contraction or expansion of the repertoire measured.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** An attack on inaccessible hammer swing space may open a different way to transmit force or maintain the connection. If no alternative actuation or connection resource exists, the correct output can be no opening.

**Comparison to prepare:** Same criticism with and without the opening-to-construction obligation; inspect any earlier builder idea.

**Defeat condition:** The builder only repairs the incumbent, substitutes another problem, or depends on an unsupported variable.

**Relationship to existing work:** An explicit research refinement of the user's destructive-to-creative tactic and existing R compositions. It is not claimed as a new attack principle.

**Academic rationale:** [A17][A17], [A24][A24]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC13. Turn an alleged necessity into a constrained countermodel

**Bottleneck:** A customary tool is treated as logically necessary for the valued function.

**Operative move:** Type the premise, distinguish implication from necessity, then search for a source-feasible case where the function holds without the alleged necessary tool.

**Procedure and handoff:** Lane A formalizes the necessity claim; Lane B searches for a countermodel. The attack checks logical validity and real admissibility separately. The builder supplies an operation satisfying the invariant under the missing-tool condition. Distill carries the typed premise, countermodel and any unresolved feasibility gap.

**Required artifact:** Claim type, admissible countermodel or no-opening proof, construction and separating test.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** The target is a required connection, not the verb hammering. A non-impact connection refutes hammer necessity only if it meets the supplied connection requirements.

**Comparison to prepare:** Ordinary reframing versus typed necessity challenge with an explicit countermodel obligation.

**Defeat condition:** An inverse or converse is presented as a valid deduction; the countermodel changes the function or invents resources.

**Relationship to existing work:** Strengthens earlier logical inversion with a typed countermodel and novelty comparison. Academic search-space theory motivates exploration; elementary logical validity is a separate check.

**Academic rationale:** [A03][A03], [A01][A01]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC14. Preserve an independent pool before exchanging dissent

**Bottleneck:** Early discussion synchronizes concerns before distinct mechanisms are recorded.

**Operative move:** Generate separately by nonoverlapping concerns, save the initial pool, then exchange a specific disagreement that demands a new construction.

**Procedure and handoff:** Assign concerns such as implementation resources and downstream service burden. Both receive common essential facts. Join all saved initial proposals before dialogue. The attacker points to a contradictory dependency or prediction; a builder constructs from that tension. Distill records initial mechanisms, additions after exchange and remaining disagreement.

**Required artifact:** Independent pool, tension ledger and post-exchange construction with provenance.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** One lane's attachment is easy to assemble but impossible to service under another lane's documented requirement. The exchange must construct a mechanism addressing both obligations.

**Comparison to prepare:** Immediate dialogue versus preserved independent generation then exchange, with equal total work and common facts.

**Defeat condition:** The exchange adds only consensus language, duplicates an initial mechanism, or benefits solely from extra information/time.

**Relationship to existing work:** A timing and provenance intervention for Robot Talks. Distinct concerns and actual source differences are measured rather than inferred from role names.

**Academic rationale:** [A18][A18], [A17][A17]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC15. Select on a novelty and usefulness frontier

**Bottleneck:** Generation succeeds but selection drops useful unfamiliar candidates or favors unusable surprises.

**Operative move:** Freeze a candidate pool and rate originality, task fit, burden and uncertainty separately. Compare ordinary selection with a predefined frontier rule.

**Procedure and handoff:** Use the identical pool in both conditions. Lane A assesses feasibility and usefulness; Lane B assesses originality against the full lineage. The attack challenges false independence or optimistic utility. Distill preserves both ratings and tradeoffs; selection can retain a feasible unfamiliar option without declaring it best.

**Required artifact:** Fixed-pool rating table, selected set and displaced-option ledger.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** An unfamiliar connection with uncertain service cost should remain visibly uncertain. It cannot beat a verified candidate by novelty points alone.

**Comparison to prepare:** Ordinary ranking versus predeclared multidimensional selection; generation stays fixed.

**Defeat condition:** Selected originality rises only by sacrificing required usefulness, or no valuable option is recovered after burden is counted.

**Relationship to existing work:** Targets the exposure/selection bottleneck from earlier studies. This proposal is a selection intervention, not proof of better idea generation.

**Academic rationale:** [A19][A19], [A23][A23], [A01][A01]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC16. Train a procedure and test it on unfamiliar cases

**Bottleneck:** The method only works while the current example or prompt is visible.

**Operative move:** Teach one named operation with varied practice, then test retention and transfer using cases prepared before training outcomes are seen.

**Procedure and handoff:** Use a matched active control. Test immediate assisted performance separately from delayed unassisted performance for humans. For agents, declare external retained state and test both retrieval-assisted and unassisted conditions. Attack answer leakage and surface repetition. Distill stores a method card with conditions and counterexamples, excluding held-out answers.

**Required artifact:** Curriculum, active control, predeclared holdout sets, retained-state specification and transfer results when actually run.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** Teach generic-part decomposition on several tools; test a different tool class and a workflow problem. Renaming the same hammer problem is insufficient far transfer.

**Comparison to prepare:** Procedure training versus equal-time ordinary practice, across baseline, immediate, retention and unfamiliar-task tests.

**Defeat condition:** Gains vanish after scaffolding removal, only reproduce the taught answer, or require undeclared retained state.

**Relationship to existing work:** Adds an explicit learning study, which the earlier round-specific generation experiments did not establish. No agent weight update is implied.

**Academic rationale:** [A06][A06], [A07][A07], [A08][A08], [A02][A02]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC17. Feed search failures back into the reusable method

**Bottleneck:** Successes are stored as slogans while the same search failures recur.

**Operative move:** Classify why an attempt failed, then revise one retrieval cue, applicability condition or construction obligation in the retained method.

**Procedure and handoff:** Use categories such as wrong frame, missing knowledge, fixation, invalid mutation, handoff loss and selection loss. Lane A identifies the failure from evidence; Lane B proposes one method change. Attack overfitting to the solved case. Distill retains the revised condition, counterexample and unchanged invariant. Evaluate on a separate held-out set.

**Required artifact:** Failure taxonomy, versioned method delta and later comparison plan.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** If inversion repeatedly drops a service requirement, revise the inversion card to preserve that requirement explicitly; do not insert the prior case's answer.

**Comparison to prepare:** Fixed method versus one evidence-bound method revision with matched exposure to feedback.

**Defeat condition:** Later gains only occur on the same case, the revision changes several variables, or failure is reclassified to hide it.

**Relationship to existing work:** Turns reflection into an explicit procedural-learning hypothesis. It distinguishes preserving evidence from demonstrating that adaptation improves transfer.

**Academic rationale:** [A06][A06], [A05][A05]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC18. Construct from a beneficiary's actual burden

**Bottleneck:** Alternatives optimize the producer's preferred capability while ignoring the affected person's work.

**Operative move:** Include actual beneficiary evidence, then require a candidate to preserve the valued function while reducing or redistributing a specified burden.

**Procedure and handoff:** Give all conditions the same evidence. Lane A models producer resources; Lane B traces beneficiary work. The attack identifies a hidden transfer of burden. A builder changes the operation; Distill carries the beneficiary evidence, added upkeep and uncertainty. Simulated perspectives remain hypotheses until checked.

**Required artifact:** Affected-person evidence, burden path and candidate with an observable consequence.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** A maker may prefer a permanent nail connection; the person who must dismantle or maintain it may value a different mechanism. That need must come from supplied or collected evidence.

**Comparison to prepare:** Ordinary optimization versus explicit perspective-taking instructions on the same beneficiary facts.

**Defeat condition:** The burden is invented, merely shifted to an unexamined person, or no useful operation changes.

**Relationship to existing work:** Adds user-grounded perspective taking to earlier incentive and labor-migration tactics. The research adaptation must separate new facts from new processing.

**Academic rationale:** [A25][A25], [A05][A05]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC19. Separate unfamiliarity from unresolved feasibility

**Bottleneck:** A candidate is rejected because it feels uncertain, while the rejection is described as a technical failure.

**Operative move:** Ask evaluators to record distinct reasons: demonstrated infeasibility, missing evidence, burden, outcome mismatch and unfamiliarity. Offer a bounded evidence-gathering decision for unresolved candidates.

**Procedure and handoff:** Freeze the pool and evidence. Lane A evaluates source-supported feasibility; Lane B inspects the decision reasons. The attack demands a cited reason for exclusion. Distill preserves uncertainty without converting it to either success or failure. Compare ordinary choice with this explicit reason ledger.

**Required artifact:** Candidate → rejection reason → evidence → next discriminating test, including valid technical rejections.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** For an unfamiliar connection, an unmeasured property is a missing-evidence issue; a proven requirement violation is infeasibility. The ledger does not require adopting either candidate.

**Comparison to prepare:** Ordinary evaluation versus reason-separated evaluation with identical available facts and decision stakes.

**Defeat condition:** The ledger admits objectively worse options, masks necessary rejection, or changes confidence without improving justified choice.

**Relationship to existing work:** Adds an uncertainty/acceptance test to earlier exposure strategies. It investigates rejection bias while preserving legitimate feasibility constraints.

**Academic rationale:** [A20][A20], [A19][A19]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

### AC20. Audit source diversity rather than counting agents

**Bottleneck:** Several agents produce polished versions of the same source-induced mechanism.

**Operative move:** Track which examples, relations and facts seed each candidate. Preserve pre-assistance ideas and deliberately vary retrieval paths before a joined comparison.

**Procedure and handoff:** Keep essential task facts common. Lane A records provenance and pre-example candidates; Lane B maps mechanism families. Alternative conditions use a shared seed set or distinct source routes with total source coverage and cost reported. Attack apparent diversity caused by vocabulary. Distill preserves rare feasible mechanisms and their actual dependencies.

**Required artifact:** Source/candidate graph, mechanism-family diversity and qualified usefulness table.

**Illustration, not a tested result:** Three agents shown the same nail example may agree on impact fastening. Test whether different verified source mechanisms add feasible families, rather than calling the number of agents diversity.

**Comparison to prepare:** Shared seeds versus diversified source routes under matched resources; report unavoidable information differences. Compare pool quality and cross-output similarity separately.

**Defeat condition:** Diversity is lexical, adds only infeasible ideas, or results from more information rather than the specified routing intervention.

**Relationship to existing work:** Extends independent lanes with evidence-provenance and collective-diversity measurement. The academic AI finding is from human story writing, so agent transfer remains a hypothesis.

**Academic rationale:** [A22][A22], [A12][A12], [A18][A18]. Reading scope and evidence limits are in the source register.

## Two bounded prototype investigations to prepare first

### Prototype A: Can constructive attack expand a property-based search?

Use CX01. Supply a closed case with actual properties, hard constraints and at least one permissible alternate mechanism. Prepare adverse cases in which no alternative is available. Compare ordinary analysis, generic-parts search, constructive attack and their composition.

Use the number of **qualified, locally distinct mechanisms** as a candidate primary endpoint, with task outcome and burden reported separately. Compare every final card with the complete source lineage. The study must be able to produce an honest no-opening result.

Do not credit the composition for an idea already present in AC01's initial inventory or proposal. No prototype trial was run during this extension.

### Prototype B: Does a strategy persist beyond the current round?

Use CX06. Train one operation, preferably AC01 or AC02, with an active practice comparator. Prepare retention and transfer cases in advance. Compare a fixed method card with a card revised only from training failures.

For humans, distinguish assisted use from later use without the training prompt. For agents, declare the exact retained artifact and retrieval condition. Evaluate method choice, useful construction and refusal on unfamiliar cases. A later successful call with the same worked example is not evidence of transfer.

Neither prototype specifies a confirmatory sample size. Choose that from the intended precision, outcome variability and comparison structure before execution; a small coverage pilot cannot substitute for it.

## What the catalog adds

The existing work concentrated on generating and carrying an alternative through one analysis episode. This portfolio adds academic mechanisms, candidate controls, a separate selection question, and procedures for investigating retention and transfer.

A strategy can now fail in several informative ways: it may not apply, add nothing beyond the inspected repertoire, be infeasible, be useful only with scaffolding, fail to transfer, or shift burden to somebody else. Those are distinct results. Keeping them distinct makes future method development possible.
