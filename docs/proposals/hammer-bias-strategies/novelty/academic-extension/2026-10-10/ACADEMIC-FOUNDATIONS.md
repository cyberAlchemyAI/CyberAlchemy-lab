## 1. Direct answer

Creative performance can be developed. Training studies support improvements in measured performance, although the size, durability and transfer of those gains depend on what is trained, what is tested and study quality. Creativity also involves construction: people use prior knowledge to generate, transform, explore and evaluate possibilities. Learning and creating therefore describe interacting processes rather than mutually exclusive explanations. [Scott et al., 2004][A06]; [Sio & Lortie-Forgues, 2024][A07].

There is no established universal list of ingredients that guarantees a creative result. Under the influential standard definition, **originality and effectiveness are required to classify a result as creative**. Those are criteria for a result, not a causal recipe for producing it. Effectiveness can mean task fit or appropriateness; it need not mean commercial value. [Runco & Jaeger, 2012][A01].

For this research program, the practical question is: **what change in knowledge, representation, search, construction, selection or retained experience creates an additional defensible possibility?** More agents, longer analysis and more unfamiliar words do not answer that question.

The strongest preparation direction is to build a process that can vary its frame, generate a concrete alternative, test it against constraints and prior work, preserve what it learned, and use that learning on a different problem. This is our synthesis for the program; it is not an experimentally proven formula.

## 2. Review method and evidence boundaries

Searches and source checks were conducted on 10 October 2026. The search covered creativity definitions; training and enhancement meta-analyses; functional fixedness and example fixation; analogical transfer; creative cognition; flexibility and persistence; incubation; constraints; design reframing; debate and group generation; idea selection; motivation; measurement; and AI effects on diversity.

Sources were selected for explanatory relevance and evidence type. The review uses original publications, original-paper copies, and publisher or author-institution records. It includes theory, research monographs, experiments, meta-analyses and observational studies. It does not pool their findings as if they were one type of evidence.

This is a selective narrative review, not a systematic review. No exhaustive retrieval claim, formal screening count, publication coverage guarantee or new statistical meta-analysis is made. Some sources were inspected through abstracts and metadata; others through targeted original-text passages. The exact reading scope is recorded in [SOURCES.json](/home/vlad_/projects/CyberAlchemy-lab/docs/proposals/hammer-bias-strategies/novelty/academic-extension/2026-10-10/SOURCES.json). The complete Geneplore book and every cited article were not read in full.

Four boundaries govern the interpretation:

- A theory proposes mechanisms; it does not automatically prove them.
- Observational association does not establish intervention effects.
- An intervention effect on a human task does not automatically transfer to an agent workflow.
- An academic mechanism can justify preparing a strategy without establishing that our adaptation works.

The 20 cards below the research directory are **author-proposed operational adaptations**. Their precise procedures, combinations, comparisons and defeat conditions were designed for this project. Citations support the underlying rationale, not efficacy of those complete procedures.

## 3. What counts as novelty?

### 3.1 Specify who already knows what

A result can be new to a learner, new to a team's inspected repertoire, new to a professional field, or historically unprecedented. Those claims require different comparisons. Boden distinguishes psychological from historical creativity; the Four C model separately distinguishes learning-level, everyday, professional and eminent creativity. These are related frameworks, not identical category systems. [Boden, 2009][A03]; [Kaufman & Beghetto, 2009][A02].

For the hammer problem, discovering a removable connection can be valuable to the person who only considered nails even if the mechanism is well known elsewhere. The report should describe that as a newly available alternative for the present repertoire. A field-original invention would additionally require a credible domain-wide prior-art investigation.

### 3.2 Distinguish four outputs

| Output | What changed? | What remains to establish? |
|---|---|---|
| New wording | Description or metaphor. | A changed operation, explanation or usable test. |
| New candidate | A representation or arrangement absent from the inspected set. | Fit, resources and whether it duplicates upstream work. |
| Creative contribution | An original and appropriate contribution for the stated context. | Strength of the evidence and breadth of the novelty comparison. |
| Innovation | Successful implementation of a creative idea in a relevant setting. | Actual adoption, performance and consequences. |

The creativity/innovation distinction is used in organizational research. Our earlier finite-model predictions do not count as implementation in that organizational sense. [Amabile & Pratt, 2016][A05].

### 3.3 Novelty has several mechanisms

Boden's taxonomy distinguishes combining familiar elements, exploring an existing conceptual space, and changing the rules defining that space. It offers three research targets: search more effectively inside the current model; connect currently separate mechanisms; or modify a nonessential rule so a previously excluded construction becomes expressible. [Boden, 2009][A03].

A transformational proposal must still preserve the user's essential outcome. Replacing a hard requirement with an easier one is scope change, not evidence of a superior solution. The hard/soft distinction must be settled from actual source facts.

## 4. Can creativity be learned?

### 4.1 The evidence supports development, with important limits

| Academic synthesis | Main finding checked | Interpretation for this program |
|---|---|---|
| Scott, Leritz & Mumford, 2004 | Review of 70 studies; stronger programs emphasized cognitive skills, applicable heuristics and realistic exercises. | Train an identifiable operation and practice its use, rather than issuing a generic creativity instruction. |
| Sio & Lortie-Forgues, 2024 | 169 studies and 844 effects; estimated average 0.53 SD before bias adjustment, with adjusted estimates of 0.29–0.32 SD; serious quality concerns. | Expect conditional gains; retain negative results and measure unfamiliar-task transfer. |
| Haase, Hanel & Gronau, 2025; online first 2023 | 332 effects; average Hedges' g = 0.53, 95% CI [0.44, 0.61], with substantial variation. This analysis did not detect publication bias. | Enhancement can help measured performance, but benefits depend on the method and context. |

Sources: [Scott et al.][A06], [Sio & Lortie-Forgues][A07], [Haase et al.][A08].

The two recent syntheses are not independent guarantees of the same effect. They examine different scopes and analyses. The difference in publication-bias findings is material: a failure to detect bias does not prove absence, while adjusted estimates depend on their assumptions. The 2024 review provides a reason to be cautious about the larger headline estimate.

The practical conclusion is that measured performance is trainable. These summaries do not establish universal far transfer, permanent gains, or a guaranteed path from a classroom technique to eminent discovery.

### 4.2 What could be learned?

For this program, trainable targets are operational hypotheses:

1. Recognize when a tool has entered the goal definition.
2. Describe parts through their properties rather than customary use.
3. Retrieve and map a relation from another domain.
4. Alter a representation while preserving its constraints.
5. Alternate broad search with focused development.
6. Convert a criticism into a permissible construction variable.
7. Select an original candidate without discarding usefulness.
8. Recognize where a technique does not apply.
9. Store a mechanism with its conditions and failure cases.
10. Transfer the mechanism to a task whose surface vocabulary differs.

This list is our proposed curriculum, informed by the mechanisms in sections 5–7. It is not a validated training program.

### 4.3 Separate four kinds of improvement

| Improvement | Required observation | Common false substitute |
|---|---|---|
| Better performance during an intervention | Matched task comparison with the intervention present. | More fluent prose. |
| Retained procedural learning | Later performance after immediate scaffolding is removed. | Repeating the worked example. |
| Transfer | Improvement on unfamiliar cases with changed surface features and matched resources. | Recognition of the same answer in different wording. |
| Persistent system learning | A documented retained-state or policy change that supports later improvement. | Calling a stateless model again. |

Prompting a model, giving it a stored card, updating an external memory, and changing its model weights are different interventions. No weight update or persistent learning was performed in this extension. An artifact can support future retrieval; its existence does not demonstrate transfer.

## 5. Ingredients for emergence: requirements, mechanisms and enabling conditions

### 5.1 Do not turn an evidence map into a universal recipe

Amabile's componential theory explicitly proposes domain skills, creativity-relevant processes, intrinsic task motivation and the surrounding environment as necessary components within its model. This is a useful organizing theory. It should be identified as such rather than presented as an experimentally settled law covering every creator and context. [Amabile, 2012][A04].

The following table separates definitional requirements from facilitating conditions and research-specific requirements.

| Ingredient or condition | Status | Why it matters here | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Originality relative to a stated comparison | Definitional criterion. | Establishes what is actually being added. | Same mechanism already exists in the inspected repertoire. |
| Appropriateness to a purpose or context | Definitional criterion. | Prevents novelty from becoming irrelevant variation. | Proposal changes the required outcome or fails task fit. |
| Relevant knowledge and usable materials | Componential-theory input; strong practical rationale. | Provides elements and enough understanding to build. | Attractive analogy depends on an unavailable capability. |
| A searchable degree of freedom | Requirement of this intervention program. | Allows a dependency, rule, representation or action to vary. | Every proposed mutation violates a fixed constraint. |
| Generation and exploration processes | Cognitive theory and task evidence. | Produces candidates and discovers what they could do. | Analysis never reaches a construction. |
| Flexible search and persistent development | Supported pathways in creative-ideation research. | Enables both mechanism diversity and completion. | Many undeveloped sketches, or one endlessly polished incumbent. |
| Motivation, autonomy, meaningful work and useful feedback | Conditional social and motivational influences. | Supports sustained human investigation and revision. | Approval seeking replaces examination of alternatives. |
| Resources, time and informative constraints | Context-dependent enabling conditions. | Makes trials possible and focuses search. | Deprivation removes every viable experiment. |
| Exchange, dissent and permission to expose failure | Conditional team influences. | Can bring different evidence into the search. | Participants suppress observations or synchronize too early. |
| Selection and domain assessment | Requirement for judging contribution; empirical selection issue. | Distinguishes useful additions from merely surprising candidates. | An unusual but unusable option wins by novelty alone. |
| Retained experience and unfamiliar-case transfer | Requirement for claiming learning in this program. | Turns an episode into a reusable capability. | Benefit disappears once a worked example is removed. |

Academic support is developed in the next sections. The failure signals and program requirements are our design decisions.

### 5.2 Knowledge supplies material and can also constrain search

The associative account treats creativity as connecting existing elements under requirements. Creative cognition likewise offers generation and exploration as ordinary cognitive processes. This explains why novelty need not come from nowhere. [Mednick, 1962][A26]; [Finke et al., 1992][A09].

Experience has a second effect: it can make familiar features easier to retrieve than alternatives. Smith and colleagues found conformity to supplied examples in creative-generation tasks, including when participants were told to differ. The lesson is to investigate what examples make salient and what they hide. It is not to remove expertise or all examples. [Smith et al., 1993][A12].

A useful balance is a grounded core with a controlled unfamiliar connection. Uzzi and colleagues observed this pattern in highly cited scientific papers. Their bibliometric result is an association with citation impact, not causal proof that adding unusual citations creates good science. [Uzzi et al., 2013][A21].

### 5.3 A variable must be available

A creative process can be open while its essential purpose stays fixed. Changeable variables might include the carrier of information, representation of a part, sequence of actions, interface, distribution of work, nonessential convention, or the question asked of a measurement.

Constraints are heterogeneous. Resources, deadlines, rules and requirements can focus search or impede it through different mechanisms. A recipe such as “add constraints” is therefore too blunt. [Acar et al., 2019][A15].

Our preparation rule is to label each constraint as fixed, convention, preference, or unknown. Unknown does not mean freely changeable.

### 5.4 Broad search and sustained construction have different jobs

The dual-pathway model describes originality through both category flexibility and persistent search within categories. There is no implication that only loose association is creative. [Nijstad et al., 2010][A13].

Our corresponding design hypothesis uses two explicit budgets: one for finding different mechanisms and one for making a selected mechanism work. The allocation is a new experimental variable, not a ratio supplied by the paper.

For the hammer scenario, four plausible connection mechanisms can be a breadth result. Accounting for service access, materials and a separating load case for one mechanism is a persistence result. Both still need actual facts before feasibility can be claimed.

## 6. Social and motivational conditions

### 6.1 Debate can open search; hostility can close it

Nemeth and colleagues compared debate-permitting instructions with traditional brainstorming instructions in US and French groups. Their published abstract reports a general benefit for debate, but the working-paper results show that individual contrasts did not all reach significance. The evidence concerns particular group tasks and generation measures. [Nemeth et al., 2004][A17].

Amabile's theory also identifies harsh criticism as a possible barrier. There is no contradiction in distinguishing examination of an idea from interpersonal threat. Team psychological safety was associated with learning behavior in Edmondson's field study; this supports attention to the conditions under which people reveal errors, not a guarantee of creative output. [Amabile, 2012][A04]; [Edmondson, 1999][A24].

The project hypothesis is consequently specific: **attack a dependency, preserve a valued function, expose a permitted variable, and require a builder to construct from it.** Academic debate evidence supports investigating the role of disagreement. The complete attack-to-construction contract is our adaptation and remains untested in this extension.

### 6.2 Independent generation and exchange solve different problems

Brainstorming research identifies production blocking as a major source of losses in interactive human groups. Exchange can also stimulate further ideas. [Stroebe et al., 2010][A18].

A prepared Robot Talks route can therefore preserve initial independent proposals before exchange, then ask for a new construction from a concrete conflict between concerns. Separate role names alone do not establish independent thinking, and human production-blocking mechanisms should not be presumed identical to agent orchestration.

### 6.3 The beneficiary helps define usefulness

Grant and Berry's field and laboratory studies connect prosocial motivation and perspective taking to the relation between intrinsic motivation and creativity. The practical adaptation is to bring an affected person's actual needs and burdens into evaluation. A simulated role can generate hypotheses, but cannot establish what a real person experiences. [Grant & Berry, 2011][A25].

The organizational componential update includes meaning, progress and synergistic extrinsic motivation. It does not support a blanket rule that every reward is harmful. [Amabile & Pratt, 2016][A05].

### 6.4 Acceptance is a separate bottleneck

Mueller and colleagues found that uncertainty could activate bias against creative ideas and impair recognition. Rietzschel and colleagues found selection favored feasibility and desirability over originality; asking for original selections could increase originality while lowering rated effectiveness. [Mueller et al., 2012][A20]; [Rietzschel et al., 2010][A19].

Our response is to test selection rules on an already fixed candidate pool. A novelty-generating method should not receive credit for an effect caused solely by presenting or ranking its ideas differently.

## 7. Mechanisms that support preparing new strategies

| Mechanism | Academic basis | Proposed operational change | What must not be inferred |
|---|---|---|---|
| Functional unfixing | Generic-parts experiment, A11. | Decompose tool components into properties before proposing uses. | Every strange use is effective. |
| Relational transfer | Two-analogy schema induction, A10. | Compare two mechanisms, extract the relation, then map it to the target. | A distant metaphor is sufficient. |
| Provisional construction | Creative cognition, A09; process description in A12. | Generate bounded structures before fixing their interpretation. | Unconstrained randomness guarantees invention. |
| Rule transformation | Boden, A03. | Change one nonessential generative rule while preserving the purpose. | Removing a requirement is a solution. |
| Problem/solution co-evolution | Designer protocol study, A16. | Let a trial disclose a hidden framing assumption and revise the frame explicitly. | Any convenient reframing is legitimate. |
| Breadth and depth | Dual-pathway model, A13. | Allocate separate search and development passes. | More categories are always better. |
| Incubation | Meta-analysis, A14. | Test prepared human interruption conditions; test fresh-context agent conditions separately. | A sleeping model incubates a problem. |
| Constraint mechanisms | Integrative review, A15. | Vary one declared soft constraint with informative measurements. | Scarcity automatically makes people creative. |
| Debate | Group experiments, A17. | Convert a specific counterexample into a construction variable. | Humiliation or indiscriminate negativity is productive. |
| Independent pool and exchange | Brainstorming review, A18. | Preserve proposals before mutual influence, then measure additions after exchange. | Agent count measures cognitive diversity. |
| Separate selection | Selection experiments, A19; measurement study, A23. | Hold the pool fixed and compare explicit selection rules. | Fluency, rarity or consensus alone establishes creativity. |
| Source diversity under AI assistance | Short-story experiment, A22. | Preserve pre-assistance candidates and inspect mechanism-level convergence. | The story-writing finding proves engineering-agent homogenization. |

These are mechanisms to investigate, not a ranking of methods.

## 8. Why this matters for agent-assisted analysis

In Doshi and Hauser's randomized short-story study, access to AI ideas improved evaluated output while increasing similarity between writers' stories. Individual quality and collective variety can therefore move in different directions in that setting. [Doshi & Hauser, 2024][A22].

For our program, this motivates measuring diversity among **feasible mechanisms**, rather than only counting words or ideas. Different personas, temperatures, role labels or repeated calls can be useful manipulations, but they do not prove distinct evidence or mechanism repertoires.

Four transfer boundaries remain explicit:

1. Human motivation findings suggest how to organize human participants; they do not establish feelings in agents.
2. Human incubation involves a person between attempts; a new context is a separate computational intervention.
3. Agent improvement within one prompted workflow is different from learning a reusable procedure.
4. Agreement among model reviewers is different from independently established usefulness or field originality.

The existing report's upstream-duplication finding is especially relevant. A later fresh-builder card can look new even when the same operation appeared in an earlier component proposal. The new source ledger and strategy cards retain full comparison lineage as a research requirement.

## 9. Integrating logical inversion and destructive-to-creative reasoning

### 9.1 Inversion should create a search hypothesis

From “if a hammer is used, a nail can be driven,” neither “only hammers can drive nails” nor “without a hammer the required function is impossible” follows.

There are at least three different moves:

- **Logical check:** test whether the statement was an implication, equivalence, necessary condition or empirical generalization. A valid contrapositive preserves the meaning of a genuine implication; an inverse or converse generally does not.
- **Search inversion:** ask whether the required function is attainable without the assumed capability.
- **Causal inversion:** investigate whether the tool or its incentives help generate the very demand it appears to serve.

These distinctions continue the earlier report's formal reasoning. The academic extension provides a search-space account for testing them; it does not turn invalid inference into a creative technique.

A completed inversion must supply a countermodel, alternative construction, discriminating observation or precise no-opening result. A reversed slogan is insufficient.

### 9.2 Destruction should expose material for construction

The attack contract is:

```text
Failure: what dependency or assumption fails under which supported condition?
Invariant: what valued function must survive?
Opening: which source-supported variable can change?
Construction: what operation uses that variable?
Defeat: what equivalence, observation or resource failure rejects the addition?
```

The crucial expansion is the opening. Attacks that merely make the existing hammer better can be useful engineering, but do not demonstrate an expanded mechanism repertoire.

An attack that uncovers no permissible variable is still informative. A source-backed refusal should survive the handoff; the builder must not silently substitute an easier problem.

## 11. How to investigate the ingredients

### 11.1 Diagnose the bottleneck before adding workflow

| Observed bottleneck | First strategy to investigate | Closest comparator |
|---|---|---|
| Every option retains the customary tool function. | AC01 generic parts. | Ordinary whole-object descriptions. |
| Analogies are striking but have no transferable operation. | AC02 two-source relational mapping. | Same sources without comparison/mapping instructions. |
| The frame excludes all alternatives by convention. | AC03 rule transformation or AC06 co-evolution. | Frozen ordinary frame with equal information. |
| Many categories appear but nothing is developed. | AC07 breadth/depth allocation. | Equal-budget breadth-only and depth-only conditions. |
| Critique improves the incumbent but adds no mechanism. | AC12 constructive attack. | Same attack without an opening-to-construction obligation. |
| New candidates exist but final selection drops them. | AC15 selection frontier. | Fixed pool with ordinary selection. |
| The benefit only exists while the worked example is visible. | AC16 retention and transfer. | Matched active practice with equal contact time. |
| Different assistants converge on the same mechanisms. | AC20 source diversity. | Equal-budget agents sharing the same initial examples. |

This table is a routing proposal. It makes no claim about which intervention will win.

### 11.2 Test composition against its components

For a recipe A → B, compare ordinary analysis, A alone, B alone and A → B using the same source information and explicit resource budgets. Randomize assignment and counterbalance case order where possible. Fix criteria and handoff rules before generation.

The evaluation must inspect:

- Actual application of the intended mechanism.
- Feasibility under supplied constraints.
- Addition beyond ordinary analysis, each component, and relevant upstream proposals.
- Useful outcomes, burden and displaced work.
- Missing comparisons and unavailable controls.
- Actual elapsed time, model calls and token use where available.

Multiple cards from one case or one source pool are not independent replications. The unit of analysis and any repeated-measure or clustered structure must be declared. A small pilot can establish coverage or measurement feasibility; it cannot support broad efficacy claims simply because it has several role outputs.

### 11.3 Investigate learning with held-out cases

Prepare distinct training, retention, near-transfer and far-transfer cases before seeing outcomes. Near transfer changes surface form while preserving important relations; far transfer changes the domain and may require a different abstraction. Declare that distinction in advance.

Use an active control with comparable practice, examples, time and feedback. Where feasible, randomly assign participants or independent system configurations, collect baseline measures, and evaluate later outputs with condition-masked qualified raters. Test both performance and whether participants choose the method appropriately. A trained refusal can be correct.

For human participants, a later retention test may be conducted after a declared interval. For agents, explicitly identify retained state: external cards, retrieval rules, planner policy, or model updates. Evaluate retrieval assistance separately from unassisted transfer. Keep held-out answers and prior grades out of the retained artifact.

If a study only improves immediate prompted output, report that result. Do not rename it learning.

### 11.4 Test selection with a fixed pool

Freeze the candidate pool and compare selection conditions on the same alternatives. Record originality, task fit, burden and uncertainty separately before combining scores. A predefined Pareto rule is one proposal: retain candidates that are not dominated across the stated dimensions. It is a project rule, not an academic theorem of optimal creativity.

Subjective judgments need clear instructions and reliability assessment. Silvia and colleagues examined more defensible scoring approaches for divergent-thinking tasks, while also documenting problems with traditional scoring. Measurement design must fit the present task; raw rarity counts can confound fluency with originality. [Silvia et al., 2008][A23].

### 11.5 Failure is evidence; absence of a control is not a zero effect

Use separate statuses for valid no-opening, not-applied, unknown, infeasible and measured no-benefit. Preserve the first scored output and original judgments. A later case that supplies a missing control is a new study, following the separation already established by the earlier coverage work.

For inference, predeclare the primary endpoint, effect-size calculation, uncertainty interval, missing-data policy and multiple-comparison handling. Determine a sample from the intended precision or a justified power calculation before a confirmatory run. No such sample-size calculation or trial was performed here.
