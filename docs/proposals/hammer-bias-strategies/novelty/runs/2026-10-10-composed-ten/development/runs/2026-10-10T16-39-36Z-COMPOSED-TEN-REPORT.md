## What survives the novelty check

R04 adds a delivered construction in both cases. In development, the fresh builder reconstructs a rejected all-manual migration as one early hand-carrier bridge plus two powered batches. Both this route and its strongest supplied rival release twelve correct carriers by minute13 with seven occupied transfer-clerk minutes. Arrival timing differs. A one-boundary bridge delay predicts eleven releases. A direct deterministic calculation supports those model predictions, with no observed trial or ranking advantage.

In the second case, R04’s main six-action request schedule already appears in original S04 stage1 and stage2. The concrete bulk4-to3 boundary attack differs; the migration, burden accounting and general attack do not become new merely because they are recombined. The mechanical final-repertoire score stays unchanged.

R06 adds a delivered construction in the second case only. It advances N8 validation to tick2 and puts it behind N5 in the tick4 bulk queue. The inspected ordinary/S08/S05 upstream chains do not contain this exact viable continuation. Finite replay predicts first N8 delivery at tick6 versus tick8 for the late-validation rival; both reach eight registrations and recognitions within six actions. This is a concrete change in dependency and timing, not evidence of generic superiority.
