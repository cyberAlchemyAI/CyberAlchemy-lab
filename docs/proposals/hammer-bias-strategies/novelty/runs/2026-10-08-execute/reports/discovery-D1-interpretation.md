# First discovery case: reviewed interim findings

Status: first-case review complete, second-case comparison pending. This is a descriptive interim report, not finalist selection. The frozen prompts and advancement rule remain unchanged.

The 28 attendance-export trials produced 110 raw openings. Two independent method-anonymous judges and one bounded adjudication yielded 21 canonical families: 10 locally new, five equivalent to the reference archive, and six with unresolved novelty. Thirteen unresolved entries preserve category and equivalence uncertainty. Both judges assessed all 110 openings as retained through Distill, with no Distill-only additions or unsafe recommendations identified.

Evidence: [normalized judgments](discovery-D1-resolved.json), [adjudication](../reviews/discovery-D1-adjudication.json), [J1](../reviews/discovery-D1-J1.json), [J2](../reviews/discovery-D1-J2.json). The canonical family IDs below refer to that normalized case.

## What a two-item presentation would hide

The protocol also requires a sensitivity view showing the first two distinct retained families under its frozen ordering: fit, usefulness, observability, then raw order Z1, Z2, A1, A2. Applying that ordering to the locked first-case judgments removes all eight qualifying exclusive A occurrences from view: six distinct families across seven trials. Each affected trial's first two families comes from Lane Z. These ideas were preserved in the complete Distill output.

This identifies a possible selection bottleneck: generation can add a distinction and compression can preserve it, while a later display limit hides it. The observation is a deterministic consequence of this ranking, including its incumbent-first tie order. It does not establish how other rankings or human reviewers would behave. The original main advancement rule continues to use the full qualified family sets across both cases. Evidence: [complete first-two sensitivity table](discovery-D1-first-two-sensitivity.json).

## Concrete openings

- **N04 / C19:** Treat assignment/name changes as events that identify which weeks need reconciliation. The proposed distinction is change-triggered review, with less review of unchanged cases. Both repetitions contain this mechanism. Event reliability, affordability, and practical benefit remain untested. Sources: [U002 A](../outputs/U002/A.json), [U017 A](../outputs/U017/A.json).
- **N08 / C16–C18:** State how interacting mechanisms could overcome each other's separate failures, then compare baseline, each component, and the combination. The three hypotheses concern intended-set confirmation with identity resolution, revision receipts with regeneration, and combined reassignment/rename handling. The component ideas are familiar; the proposed interaction predictions supply the assessed distinction. These are speculative hypotheses. Sources: [U007 A](../outputs/U007/A.json), [U019 A](../outputs/U019/A.json).
- **N09 / C20:** Reuse an existing completion trace as a private readiness cue. Its benefit depends on completion-to-retrieval delay actually contributing to the problem. Correct completion alone does not establish correct content. Source: [U008 A](../outputs/U008/A.json).
- **N12 / C11:** Test whether a checklist reduces verification work even when CSV contents are already correct. The checklist is familiar; separating its checking benefit from its error-correction benefit is the locally new question. Both repetitions contain it. Sources: [U014 A](../outputs/U014/A.json), [U015 A](../outputs/U015/A.json).

These findings identify candidate causal distinctions and discriminating questions. They do not demonstrate worldwide originality, strategy superiority, transfer, or real-world benefit. Finalist selection still requires the independently reviewed second discovery case under the original rule.
