# Strategy cards for generating novelty

Prepared operators. Examples are hypothetical candidate sketches. Assess novelty against a frozen case-specific archive and controls; no global originality or effectiveness is established.

## N01 Move the function

**Question:** Who or what else could perform the essential function?

**Assumption to transform:** The familiar tool or specialist must carry the function.

**Operation:** Move the function into the material, interface, artifact, or environment; name the replacement causal path.

**Hammer example:** A component geometry that grips its mate, reducing the fastening work required during assembly.

**Analysis example:** A change carries evidence bound to its exact contents, reducing reconstruction of its assurance case.

**Distinctive change:** Function carrier and causal path

**Reject superficial novelty:** Delegating unchanged work to another similarly equipped actor.

**Probe:** Compare objects with and without the relocated function under identical outcome requirements.

**Relationship to earlier work:** S02 and S15: makes the different mechanism explicit.

## N02 Find demand created by the solution

**Question:** Which demand is being generated by the way our solution organizes the world?

**Assumption to transform:** The problem exists independently of the incumbent intervention.

**Operation:** Add a hypothesized feedback edge from the intervention to demand; derive a counterfactual observation.

**Hammer example:** A hammer producer standardizes components around nail holes, then reads the resulting demand as proof that nails are necessary.

**Analysis example:** Approval friction encourages larger batches, and larger batches create more difficult approvals.

**Distinctive change:** Feedback edge and its predicted observable

**Reject superficial novelty:** A slogan that bureaucracy creates bureaucracy without an observable feedback path.

**Probe:** Vary batching or an intake rule in a model or bounded study while preserving assurance; predict demand and complexity separately.

**Relationship to earlier work:** S07 and S15: extends causal reversal to an endogenous demand loop.

## N03 Remove the intermediate need

**Question:** What relationship could change so the named task no longer has to happen?

**Assumption to transform:** The intermediate task is necessary for the underlying outcome.

**Operation:** Remove a dependency by redesigning an interface, then enumerate the obligations formerly satisfied by that dependency.

**Hammer example:** Interlocking components preserve stability without a separate nail-driving operation.

**Analysis example:** An explicit compatibility boundary removes recurring coordination work while retaining required review.

**Distinctive change:** Removed dependency and replacement interface

**Reject superficial novelty:** Skipping required work or quietly weakening the outcome.

**Probe:** Replay the cases that formerly needed the task, including a boundary violation.

**Relationship to earlier work:** S12: adds a constructive mechanism for removing the need.

## N04 Turn the attack into an input

**Question:** Can the force that defeats the idea become information, energy, or control?

**Assumption to transform:** A disturbance can only be resisted or repaired.

**Operation:** Design a conversion from the adverse input to a contribution to the desired function; specify its failure boundary.

**Hammer example:** An exploratory joint uses applied load to increase grip within material limits.

**Analysis example:** Evidenced disagreement routes uncertain cases to the relevant specialist; agreement alone never proves correctness.

**Distinctive change:** Adverse-input-to-useful-function conversion

**Reject superficial novelty:** Saying learn from failure without a conversion mechanism.

**Probe:** Inject benign simulated disturbances; measure useful response, false response, and breakdown conditions.

**Relationship to earlier work:** S08 and S16: converts an attack into a functional design input.

## N05 Remove a presumed prerequisite

**Question:** If this dependency disappeared, what arrangement could still work?

**Assumption to transform:** One resource, timing condition, or central actor is indispensable.

**Operation:** Forbid one changeable dependency in a counterfactual branch; generate an alternative and list every replacement dependency.

**Hammer example:** Join components when impact is unavailable, exploring tension, geometry, or external compression.

**Analysis example:** Investigate bounded precommitted decisions while a central reviewer is unavailable, only within existing authority.

**Distinctive change:** Missing dependency and explicit replacement mechanism

**Reject superficial novelty:** Restoring the dependency through a renamed actor, or treating mandatory live consent as optional.

**Probe:** Remove the dependency in a fixture and check the entire contract. A hard requirement may make the branch infeasible.

**Relationship to earlier work:** S06 and S15: a deliberate extension, not a claim of a newly invented method.

## N06 Change the unit of the problem

**Question:** What appears when we study a relation, trajectory, or subclaim rather than the current object?

**Assumption to transform:** The current object, time horizon, and boundary are the right units.

**Operation:** Change one representational dimension and translate back to concrete actions with a recomposition rule.

**Hammer example:** Maintain a relation between components across changing loads instead of optimizing a single fastening event.

**Analysis example:** Track claim validity through revisions; reopen all dependent claims when a cross-cutting change occurs.

**Distinctive change:** State variable, granularity, trigger, or recomposition rule

**Reject superficial novelty:** Renaming a task as an ecosystem without changing observations or actions.

**Probe:** Translate three contrasting cases, including a coupled failure, back to the original requirements.

**Relationship to earlier work:** S01 and S10: explicitly generates a different representation rather than only preserving one.

## N07 Transfer a distant mechanism

**Question:** Which unlike system has a relationship we could adapt?

**Assumption to transform:** Relevant solutions must resemble the current domain.

**Operation:** Map a source mechanism to the target, including an explicit failed correspondence and changed material assumptions.

**Hammer example:** Explore distributed lacing tension instead of concentrated penetrating fasteners.

**Analysis example:** Explore reservation or admission mechanisms for scarce review attention, only where obligations map.

**Distinctive change:** Transferred relation and target-specific adaptation

**Reject superficial novelty:** A decorative analogy or an unsupported claim that the source proves target performance.

**Probe:** Test a small mapped mechanism and its weakest correspondence under the same constraints.

**Relationship to earlier work:** S09: adds explicit mechanism generation and failed-correspondence tracking.

## N08 Create a new interaction between mechanisms

**Question:** What could two partial solutions do together that neither can do alone?

**Assumption to transform:** Candidate families must compete as complete alternatives.

**Operation:** Cross two mechanisms at a named interface and predict an interaction benefit.

**Hammer example:** A temporary alignment fixture hands geometry to a reversible load-bearing joint and is then removed.

**Analysis example:** A dependency map and an uncertainty signal jointly route only affected uncertain claims for focused review.

**Distinctive change:** New interface and interaction capability

**Reject superficial novelty:** A bundle of features whose benefits simply add, or a simpler parent that already performs the whole function.

**Probe:** Compare the hybrid with both parents using the same cases; inspect the claimed interaction and its added burden.

**Relationship to earlier work:** Adds an explicit recombination operator to the prior tournament.

## N09 Activate an unused affordance

**Question:** What existing property, trace, or by-product could do another job?

**Assumption to transform:** Existing resources have only their currently assigned use.

**Operation:** Bind one available property to a new operation and verify provenance before claiming it is available.

**Hammer example:** A permitted deformation mark becomes an indicator of a particular load-path failure.

**Analysis example:** Revision traces locate which changed dependency invalidated an earlier assurance claim.

**Distinctive change:** Existing property-to-new-operation relation

**Reject superficial novelty:** Requesting nonexistent data, or calling more measurement an affordance without a mechanism.

**Probe:** Seed both targeted and irrelevant changes; check whether the proposed operation distinguishes them.

**Relationship to earlier work:** Extends evidence generation with reuse of an overlooked resource.

## N10 Change what success rewards

**Question:** What would the provider invent if paid for the maintained outcome?

**Assumption to transform:** Using or selling more of the familiar tool is the right local reward.

**Operation:** Change a proposed incentive in a model; derive the resulting choices and possible gaming.

**Hammer example:** Pay for a connection that stays sound and can be repaired, rather than for the number of hammer blows or hammers sold.

**Analysis example:** Reward timely justified decisions subject to coverage and rework checks, rather than review volume alone.

**Distinctive change:** Incentive-to-behavior path

**Reject superficial novelty:** Replacing one metric with another while assuming behavior follows, or optimizing away assurance.

**Probe:** Use contrasting scenarios to test predicted choices and explicit gaming cases before any real policy change.

**Relationship to earlier work:** Parent addition prompted by an illustrative sentence in the prior exchange; hypotheses only.

## N11 Protect the anomaly

**Question:** Which awkward case could teach us a different mechanism?

**Assumption to transform:** Outliers should be averaged away or patched immediately.

**Operation:** Preserve a sourced anomaly; generate competing mechanisms that explain both ordinary and anomalous cases.

**Hammer example:** A joint that loosens during thermal movement suggests separate axes for permitted movement and forbidden separation.

**Analysis example:** A queue case whose delay persists after added capacity prompts hypotheses about rework or evidence decay.

**Distinctive change:** New explanatory distinction and discriminating prediction

**Reject superficial novelty:** Inventing an observation, treating noise as a pattern, or moving the failure elsewhere.

**Probe:** Compare normal and anomalous conditions; reject explanations that cannot distinguish them.

**Relationship to earlier work:** S03 and S08: gives an unresolved anomaly a bounded generative role.

## N12 Work backward from a future object

**Question:** What capabilities would make this surprising object possible?

**Assumption to transform:** Only currently familiar objects should enter the search.

**Operation:** Describe one concrete speculative capability, expand its dependencies, then extract a presently testable partial capability.

**Hammer example:** A joint indicates which segment of its load path is deteriorating; first test one visible indicator.

**Analysis example:** An approval identifies the exact evidence that would revoke it; first test one dependency-triggered expiry.

**Distinctive change:** New capability and dependency chain

**Reject superficial novelty:** A magical future object whose missing technology or authority is hidden.

**Probe:** Test the smallest enabling capability against both a targeted failure and an innocuous variation.

**Relationship to earlier work:** Adds backcasting from an object to the previous analysis-first routes.
