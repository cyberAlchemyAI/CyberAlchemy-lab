# A concrete example of preparing novelty

In the frozen I02 A-C1 comparison, necessity inversion produced one raw opening that both independent judges qualified as locally new, feasible and distinct from ordinary and generic controls. Both post-compression reviewers judged its protected meaning fully retained. This is model-judged evidence from one supplied case, not a performed classifier evaluation, a universal originality claim or a demonstrated process advantage.

**Starting dependency:** A supported classifier-rule decision requires a confusion table.

**Inversion:** Could the decision still be supported after removing the table? Merely reversing a logical arrow is insufficient; construct the counterexample workflow.

**Replacement operation:** The proposal uses a per-record ledger of candidate-correct minus baseline-correct, aggregated overall and by slice. Keep conservative uncertainty bounds for unresolved labels and link each adjudication to its supporting record.

**Nearest rival:** Confusion tables built from the same selected rows and admissible evidence.

**Differing observation:** Do the carriers reach the same supported decisions while one constructs no confusion tables? The changed artifact/dependency supplies the contrast. Faster analysis is an additional unmeasured question.

**Separating probe:** Run both carriers on matched copies with identical deterministic selection of influential flagged rows. Keep the supplied sixteen-access limit and seventy-minute allowance. Log withheld attempts; freeze both reports before opening the supplied answer key. Compare decisions, accounting, time and whether tables were constructed. A budget cap does not guarantee an informative outcome.

**Defeat condition:** Reject the counterexample if the ledger disagrees with the correctly computed rival, if its uncertainty bounds do not support the fixed decision thresholds, or if it quietly changes the valued outcome. Reporting unresolved is permitted.

**Protected through compression:** Replacement operation, rival, same-decision/no-table contrast, selection and access limits, answer-exposure order, uncertainty bounds, defeat condition and source lineage.

The reusable preparation move is to remove a familiar analytical artifact and reconstruct the smallest information path that can still answer the original question. Apply the same card to other artifacts, then compare each new construction against its actual nearest rival. This example does not authorize changing any current frozen rubric or adding another scored study.
