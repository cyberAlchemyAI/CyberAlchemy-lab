# A strategy that creates a new experiment

Use this card before choosing the technique. It is a preparation template, not another scored study.

**Outcome to preserve:** What must still work when the familiar tool is removed?

**Assumed necessity:** Which dependency makes us think the hammer is required?

**Novelty operation:** Remove, reverse, relocate, convert, couple or protect an anomaly. State the changed operation, not merely the technique name.

**New opening:** A mechanism, hypothesis or question that changes what we can do or learn. Keep at most two live openings.

**Nearest ordinary explanation:** The best familiar account of the same evidence, including a generic creativity suggestion.

**Different predictions:** What would we observe under our opening and under the ordinary explanation? If both predict the same thing, improve the probe or retain uncertainty.

**Smallest separating probe:** One controlled change, supplied resources and the observation that matters. Say which constraint fit is still uncertain.

**Defeat condition:** What result would make us reject or revise the opening?

**Construction after attack:** For every dependency attacked, record a replacement, removal, separating test or explicit no-opening. A criticism without this residue remains criticism.

**What Distill must preserve:** The changed causal relation, rival prediction, probe, defeat condition, uncertainty and source lineage. Save independent raw judgments before compression; judge preservation afterward.

**What we can claim:** A proposed test; a distinct operation within this supplied comparison; preserved meaning; or an observed benefit. Use only the level actually supported.

For example, the synthetic hatch case says that pressing one corner lifts the opposite corner. An anomaly-driven opening could ask whether uneven force is revealing a pivoting dependency instead of insufficient overall grip. Hold the closure arrangement constant and move the point of applied pressure; compare corner displacement with an account that predicts only increased grip. A repeatable change in the lifted corner could guide a different closure design; absence of that change would weaken the pivoting explanation. This is an unscored illustration with uncertain mechanism and feasibility, not a demonstrated novel solution.

The two lanes have different jobs: one tracks outcome and constraints; the other changes dependencies and proposes probes. Robot Talks concern reports should preserve differing predictions until a probe can separate them. Agreement alone is not the exit from hammer bias.
