# Prepare strategies that can change the next experiment

The target is a new action, causal explanation or discriminating test. An unusual sentence becomes useful when it tells us what to change, what to observe and what result would defeat the idea. Novelty here means a distinction from the supplied ordinary archive and concurrent controls; it does not establish global originality.

The smallest working artifact is an **operation–rival–defeat card**:

> Preserve **this outcome**. Change **this dependency or operation**. Our explanation predicts **X**; the nearest rival predicts **Y**. Run **this bounded probe** with the supplied resources. Drop or revise the idea if **this result** occurs.

Keep its assumptions, constraint fit and source IDs. “No viable opening” is a valid result. A logical restatement, renamed method or unsupported invention does not earn novelty credit.

These preparation questions translate the existing frozen portfolio; they add no scored study or replacement case.

| Investigation | Question that opens possibilities | Required comparison or artifact |
| --- | --- | --- |

| I02 Necessity inversion and feedback | Can the outcome survive without the hammer? What is the hammer teaching the world to need? | Separate removal of an assumed necessity from testing a return arrow that creates future tool demand. A contrapositive alone earns no mechanism credit. |
| I03 Attack to expand | What replacement becomes possible when this dependency fails? | Every attacked incumbent dependency yields a replacement, removal, separating test or explicit no-branch. Compare ordinary development, critique and constructive attack. |
| I04 Conversion and carrier | Can the disturbance become a usable signal? Who or what could carry the function instead? | Conversion and carrier changes are separate factors. Test false signals, cancellation, unchanged constraints and displaced work. |
| I05 Interface creation | What can the connection do that neither parent can do alone? | Preserve the parents, break only the interface, and predict the changed observation. Compare intact coupling with co-presence and the broken link. |
| I06 Backcasting | If the outcome were achieved without the assumed tool, what must have changed first? | Build a causal path backward to one feasible intervention and a test against an ordinary path. A preferred future without a discriminating probe is insufficient. |
| I07 Protected anomaly | Which awkward fact should design our next experiment? | Keep the supplied anomaly explicit; change a prediction or intervention because of it. Compare ordinary analysis and generic creativity. |
| I08 Deferred convergence | What should the agents predict differently before they agree? | Use identical concern reports. Preserve opposed decision commitments and select a separating probe. Compare immediate consensus with deferred convergence; this tests a component of Robot Talks. |
| I09 Faithful Distill | Which sentence could defeat the idea, and did compression keep it? | Retain the full causal relation, rival predictions, probe, defeat conditions and uncertainty. Assess additions separately. This tests preservation. |

Keep creation, retention and exposure separate; reused proposals receive no fresh creation credit. |

Preparation follows one repeatable sequence:

1. Save the valued outcome, hard constraints, supplied evidence and available resources. Identify which assumed necessity is being challenged.
2. Make two lanes: one preserves the outcome and feasibility; the other generates altered operations or hypotheses. Give concern investigators different questions.
3. Write at most two cards per generation stage. Keep every prior card's explicit retained, changed, merged, parked or rejected disposition.
4. Compare against ordinary analysis and generic creativity with the same evidence and allowance. Freeze prompts, cases, contrasts and scoring before results.
5. Use two independent assessments with administrative method labels withheld. Judge causal operations and differing predictions; preserve uncertainty about equivalence.
6. Apply Distill while protecting the test and defeat conditions. Treat summary-only ideas as new proposals requiring their own assessment.
7. Remove a method or break its interface to determine whether it contributed. Confirm informative findings on untouched cases, preserving negative and unresolved outcomes.

For the hammer problem, a useful starting card changes the joint's dependence on impact while preserving secure, removable fastening. It specifies a competing explanation, opposite observations and a bounded pull/opening probe. “Use a different tool” remains ordinary unless its operation or test differs from the comparison archive.
