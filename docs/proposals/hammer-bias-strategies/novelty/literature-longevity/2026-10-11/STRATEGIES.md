# Twelve strategies for investigating novelty and lasting appeal

**Date:** 11 October 2026. **All cards:** prepared, unrun, efficacy unknown. These are authored adaptations of the research synthesis. A source supplies a finding, framework or methodological caution; it does not validate the corresponding card. Source IDs refer to [SOURCES.json](/home/vlad_/projects/CyberAlchemy-lab/docs/proposals/hammer-bias-strategies/novelty/literature-longevity/2026-10-11/SOURCES.json).

Each card is usable as an investigation prompt. Actual experiments still require selected material, fixed conditions, participants, measurement instruments and prospective analysis. Do not count a written strategy as a successful test. Literature, manual execution and autoplay authoring have different outcomes.

## LL01 — Separate the clocks

**Question:** Which lasting effect is the idea supposed to produce?

**Inputs:** A work, mechanic or research claim; intended audience; first-encounter, repeated-use and cultural timescales.

**Operation:** Write five independent claims: initial attraction, continued engagement, revisit value, cultural transmission and delayed value. Supply an observable event for each. Rewrite every ambiguous claim about longevity using one of those events.

**Construction:** Give a short three-stage game a satisfying ending plus an optional return challenge. Its success can be completion with later reflection, continued play, or both, measured separately.

**Comparison:** Against the original single success metric of total hours or popularity. Reclassify the same hypothetical outcomes under both definitions.

**Outcome:** Whether the proposed design decisions and success classifications change once outcomes are separated.

**Defeating observation:** The decomposition changes only labels; no decision, event definition or interpretation changes. It remains useful clarification, but supplies no new mechanism.

**Basis:** LS10, LS12. **Role:** analysis and measurement preparation.

## LL02 — Locate novelty in the participant's knowledge

**Question:** New to whom, relative to what?

**Inputs:** A mechanic/text, expected audience and concrete familiarity probes.

**Operation:** List the rule, premise and interpretation separately. Ask what the participant recognizes before encounter one. Compare perceived novelty within familiarity strata instead of treating release age as a novelty measure.

**Construction:** Present the same familiar knockback rule with either a conventional damage goal or an enemy-delivery goal. Identify whether the relation, rather than the effect itself, is unfamiliar.

**Comparison:** Same underlying relation with alternate names/skins; experienced versus inexperienced audiences are measured groups, not randomized expertise.

**Outcome:** Recognition, understanding, actual strategy changes and perceived novelty scored separately.

**Defeating observation:** The alleged novelty vanishes once the old equivalent is stated, and no distinct consequence remains. Audience differences may reflect skill or preference, requiring further control.

**Basis:** LS03, LS05, LS06. **Role:** novelty diagnosis and generation.

## LL03 — Remove the surprise, retain the task

**Question:** What is valuable when the ending or rule is already known?

**Inputs:** A narrative with a reveal, or a boss with a fixed announced pattern; matched disclosure conditions.

**Operation:** Create informed and uninformed versions. For stories, disclose the ending. For gameplay, disclose the pattern without changing damage, available actions or success requirements. Treat those as separate experiments.

**Construction:** An announced finale where planning is transparent but timing and resource allocation remain demanding.

**Comparison:** A neutral length-matched introduction that does not reveal the target information. Distinguish disclosure from an additional tutorial or mechanical advantage.

**Outcome:** Enjoyment, comprehension, perceived suspense, actual performance and voluntary return.

**Defeating observation:** Knowing the relevant information removes both pleasure and remaining decisions; or benefits are explained entirely by extra instructional help. A positive result would still not establish centuries of endurance.

**Basis:** LS08, LS09. **Role:** mechanism ablation.

## LL04 — Compare encounter two with encounter one

**Question:** Does appreciation develop through reinterpretation?

**Inputs:** One selected passage/level; an explicit suspected feature; an alternative with that feature changed.

**Operation:** Record first-encounter understanding and evaluation. Revisit the same material. Ask for a changed interpretation supported by concrete details. Compare the feature-present and feature-altered versions; distinguish liking, recommendation and perceived quality.

**Construction:** A familiar encounter supports a second, evidence-backed explanation of which action caused the important consequence.

**Comparison:** Equal repeat exposure without the proposed interpretive feature. Use fresh participants per irreversible first-exposure condition.

**Outcome:** Change in evaluation and evidence-supported interpretation; optional later voluntary selection.

**Defeating observation:** Ratings rise through mere familiarity, demand or memory, while interpretation does not change; or both variants improve equally. Prompted rereading alone is not spontaneous return.

**Basis:** LS01, LS03. **Role:** literary investigation and game transfer.

## LL05 — Let the other side describe the same event

**Question:** Can a changed perspective alter responsibility, purpose or feasible action?

**Inputs:** One event sequence, two participants with conflicting goals, and an evidence ledger.

**Operation:** Describe the same events from both sides. Mark assertions, evidence and disagreements. Preserve the event sequence; then separately construct a rule variant in which the opposing purpose matters mechanically.

**Construction:** A monster seeks to deliver a charge to a hatchery. A maze delays or accelerates its arrival; neither direction is intrinsically good.

**Comparison:** The same literal goal information without character narration; and a variant where arrival has no special consequence.

**Outcome:** Changed interpretation, route selection and consequences; perspective novelty versus rule novelty are separate.

**Defeating observation:** Switching the narrator changes only sympathy or wording while every strategy and event remains identical. That may be experiential value, but cannot establish a new decision mechanism.

**Basis:** LS02, LS03, LS16. **Role:** mechanism generation through perspective.

## LL06 — Make praise and consequence disagree

**Question:** Does irony expose a neglected cost beyond its literal statement?

**Inputs:** A success claim, an actual cost ledger and an alternative action with an explicit tradeoff.

**Operation:** Write a plausible praising account contradicted by visible consequences. Translate the discrepancy into a literal causal statement. Construct one operation that addresses the cost. Preserve the rhetorical and literal versions for comparison.

**Construction:** A perfectly defended hero receives praise while the route's damaged supplies create a later disadvantage. An optional slower path preserves supplies at a visible combat cost.

**Comparison:** Identical rules and information with literal feedback; a separate variant where the later cost is absent.

**Outcome:** Cost recognition, build revision, enjoyment and decisions; test rhetorical delivery independently of the added rule.

**Defeating observation:** Irony causes confusion or only repeats what the explicit ledger already achieves; the rule variant changes no meaningful choice.

**Basis:** LS04, LS17 and prior PR/RT research. **Role:** rhetoric plus constructive generation.

## LL07 — Open one unfamiliar relation at a time

**Question:** Does comprehensible discovery sustain interest better than simultaneous unfamiliarity?

**Inputs:** Three unfamiliar rule relations, identical final content and a matched explanatory vocabulary.

**Operation:** Prepare progressive and simultaneous disclosure schedules. State every consequential rule before it can harm the player. Keep access, rewards and intended challenge comparable, documenting remaining differences.

**Construction:** Introduce enemy delivery, then its interaction with knockback, then a destination tradeoff across the three stages.

**Comparison:** Same final relations taught together; a familiar baseline controls for tutorial quality and content quantity.

**Outcome:** Comprehension, perceived novelty, usable discoveries, errors and later returns. Learning-order effects require a longitudinal comparison.

**Defeating observation:** Progressive exposure reduces useful choice or delays boredom without improving understanding or valued discovery. An advantage explained solely by extra instructional time does not isolate novelty pacing.

**Basis:** LS05, LS07. **Role:** instructional and game-design hypothesis.

## LL08 — Separate fresh content, new relations and mastery

**Question:** Which change earns another session?

**Inputs:** A fixed core game; optional cosmetic/content additions; a relation-changing rule; a challenge schedule.

**Operation:** Prepare controls for surface refresh, changed relation and continued mastery with familiar rules. Fix run length, rewards and available practice. Use factorial comparisons only where conditions can be implemented faithfully.

**Construction:** Keep damage and movement effects familiar while changing what arriving at a destination accomplishes. Compare that with a new visual map using the old relation.

**Comparison:** Familiar baseline, surface refresh, relation change; separately vary mastery opportunity or feedback. Declare confounding if difficulty cannot be matched.

**Outcome:** Voluntary return, discovery, understanding and performance growth, with pleasure measured independently.

**Defeating observation:** Return improves equally with surface refresh, or relation change helps only because it is easier or pays more. New labels do not demonstrate strategic novelty.

**Basis:** LS06, LS07, LS11. **Role:** causal study preparation.

## LL09 — Attack the assumption and preserve the investment

**Question:** Can a counter-level expand a strategy rather than erase it?

**Inputs:** A successful build, its causal dependency and the skills/items already invested.

**Operation:** Identify why the build works. Change one disclosed environmental relation. Retain a useful component and at least one adaptation route. Produce a normal-rule trace showing costs and consequences for both the unchanged and adapted build.

**Construction:** A reflect build still punishes contact, but a delivery enemy's arrival creates a hazard. Reflect remains useful while knockback or a revised route handles delivery.

**Comparison:** Ordinary new challenge with the same affordances; a blunt immunity counter is a separate comparator, not the definition of novelty.

**Outcome:** Whether a new useful plan exists, old investments contribute, players understand the counter and want another attempt.

**Defeating observation:** The old plan remains optimal unchanged, or no feasible adaptation remains. A forced equipment swap is not demonstrated creative expansion.

**Basis:** Prior destructive-to-creative/MG research; LS07 motivates measuring competence, not validating this construction. **Role:** generative attack.

## LL10 — Put the forgotten works back into the sample

**Question:** Is an alleged ingredient of classics also common in works that did not endure?

**Inputs:** A defined historical corpus, canon criteria, comparable less-canonized works and availability records.

**Operation:** Select by period, genre, language and accessibility before scoring the suspected feature. Include within-author comparisons where feasible. Document missing texts rather than silently excluding them.

**Construction:** A comparison pack of canonical and less-canonized passages, stripped of prestige labels for response assessment while retaining metadata for historical analysis.

**Comparison:** Against a classics-only inventory. Assess blind responses and institutional exposure separately.

**Outcome:** Whether a feature discriminates the defined groups and predicts reader response beyond measured exposure.

**Defeating observation:** The feature appears equally in both groups, or the effect is driven by availability and selection. Contemporary classification still cannot establish a historical causal mechanism.

**Basis:** LS10. **Role:** bias correction in research design.

## LL11 — Separate private response from transmission

**Question:** Does a work persist because people enjoy it, encounter it often, repeat a label, or recommend it?

**Inputs:** Fixed material; real exposure histories; a neutral presentation; truthful source information.

**Operation:** Measure private evaluation before recommendation. Compare neutral with accurate author/context presentation. Examine curriculum, editions and referral paths as separate observational variables. Never fabricate popularity evidence.

**Construction:** One study asks readers to choose a reread without prestige cues; another asks whether they recommend the same text after context is provided.

**Comparison:** Equal material and access across presentation conditions. Existing familiarity remains a measured confound; anonymity cannot undo prior recognition.

**Outcome:** Private liking, voluntary choice and transmission behavior, each separately reported.

**Defeating observation:** Only stated prestige changes, while private response and choices remain stable; or available exposure data cannot distinguish competing explanations.

**Basis:** LS10, LS13, LS14. **Role:** cultural-endurance investigation.

## LL12 — Allow a good ending, then measure a free return

**Question:** Does the participant stop satisfied, stop frustrated or return for another valued discovery?

**Inputs:** A fixed ending, optional replay, an observation window and access records.

**Operation:** Classify completion and reasons for departure separately from inactivity. Offer the same optional return opportunity without changing rewards or content. Measure delayed recall and interpretation even for satisfied finishers.

**Construction:** After the third boss, the player can finish or try another hero or strategy with the same rules. A comparison adds new relational content; another preserves all content.

**Comparison:** Fixed-length complete experience versus equal core experience with optional replay. Do not attribute a longer incomplete campaign's hours to higher appeal.

**Outcome:** Voluntary return at defined windows, completion, satisfaction, delayed value and interpretation. Still-active participants are incomplete observations for duration analysis.

**Defeating observation:** Added play reflects an extended endpoint, differential rewards or access; returns occur without valued discovery; satisfied completion is incorrectly coded as failure.

**Basis:** LS03, LS07, LS12. **Role:** longitudinal evaluation preparation.
