# Fourteen additional strategies for novelty in analysis

Date: 11 October 2026. IDs RT01–RT14 extend the earlier catalogs; they do not replace AC01–AC20 or change earlier study results. **All fourteen are prepared research strategies, not tested efficacy claims.** Their ingredients include established reasoning practices; their specific compositions are candidates.

For the strategic-novelty claims in this catalog, each strategy must produce an operation with a changed causal dependency and a plausible use. A striking sentence alone does not qualify. Keep an evidence lane (facts, constraints, uncertainty, precedents) and a construction lane (changed assumption, operation, consequences). An actual role can critique a construction; a named role is not by itself independent evidence.

The later [Goat Simulator case](/home/vlad_/projects/CyberAlchemy-lab/docs/proposals/hammer-bias-strategies/novelty/rhetoric-game-design/2026-10-11/GOAT-SIMULATOR-CASE.md) adds a separate target: novelty in premise, expectation and valued experience. That target requires audience and repeat-play evidence even when the available strategy is unchanged. The fourteen cards below retain their original scope and untested status.

## Catalog

### RT01 — Echo the failed promise

**Trigger:** A confident description hides its own success assumption.

**Operation:** Write the promise, its speaker, and a situation where it fails. Echo it ironically, then translate the gap into a literal causal requirement. Construct an operation that exploits that gap.

**Game construction:** “A perfectly safe route”—safe from terrain, exposed to melee. Give terrain danger and hostile contact different route weights; a retaliation build accepts contact within a health budget.

**Control and defeating observation:** Compare with a plain expectation/failure ledger using the same facts and time. Reject added value if the same route is already proposed.

**Boundary:** Irony may expose a premise; it supplies neither a proof of its negation nor an automatic solution. AR06.

### RT02 — Sarcastic praise followed by construction

**Trigger:** Criticism is accurate but leaves only rubble.

**Operation:** Praise the flawed mechanism excessively for one sentence. Immediately rewrite the criticism without sarcasm, identify the dependency, and require a builder to use one surviving useful part.

**Game construction:** “A magnificent prison—especially for its owner.” Preserve the funnel, add a timed hero exit and a sacrificial wall that an enemy charge can break.

**Control and defeating observation:** Compare sarcasm plus reconstruction with the identical literal critique plus reconstruction. Score constructions and losses separately.

**Boundary:** Aim at the design claim, not a person's worth. Human evidence is conditional and does not validate agent sarcasm. AR01.

### RT03 — Surprise with a recorded prediction

**Trigger:** An unusual idea is being called novel merely because it was unexpected.

**Operation:** Record what should happen under the incumbent model. Change one rule in a safe scenario; observe which prediction changes and what decision that enables.

**Game construction:** A weak off-beat phrase diverts a listening enemy, while an accurate phrase reinforces a corridor. Pitch error changes routing, not only damage.

**Control and defeating observation:** Compare with a random audiovisual surprise and with a numerically matched buff. Ask whether players predict the next consequence.

**Boundary:** Surprise can stimulate or impede creative performance depending on context; uncertainty alone is not discovery. AR02, AR07.

### RT04 — Resolve a contradiction through time

**Trigger:** Two desirable outcomes conflict if required at the same instant.

**Operation:** Name both requirements, their scopes and the resource preventing coexistence. Separate them into a reversible sequence with an explicit transition cost.

**Game construction:** A corridor must be closed to pursuers and open to the hero. Gate closes after the hero crosses; reopening spends stored wall energy.

**Control and defeating observation:** Compare against an always-open choke and an always-closed wall; inspect timing decisions, trapped states and energy cost.

**Boundary:** This is a design tension resolved by scoped rules, not permission to assert P and not-P as a valid inference.

### RT05 — Resolve a contradiction through space

**Trigger:** A global rule hides a useful local exception.

**Operation:** Keep the rule consistent but assign its effects to different regions, surfaces or actors. Make the boundary traversable and costly.

**Game construction:** Armor blocks direct physical hits on the front; poison exposure remains costly everywhere. Turning changes safety and retaliation opportunities.

**Control and defeating observation:** Compare with equal average reduction on all sides. Reject differentiation if facing never changes the best action.

**Boundary:** Spatial distinctions must appear in the world model and warnings, not only in the explanation.

### RT06 — Reverse the sign of an outcome

**Trigger:** The analysis assumes that a harm must always be minimized.

**Operation:** Choose a bounded harmful event, retain its cost, and add a conversion whose value depends on context. Account for its source and opportunity cost.

**Game construction:** A guarded hostile hit produces a temporary response charge. Deliberate contact is useful only while stamina and escape capacity remain sufficient.

**Control and defeating observation:** Compare with a free charge and with flat defense. If cost removal explains the gain, do not credit inversion.

**Boundary:** Do not generalize harmful is good. Reflection, on-hit rewards and risk conversion have many precedents.

### RT07 — Act through the opponent's goal

**Trigger:** Every construction acts directly on the desired output.

**Operation:** Model the opponent's observable objective and actuator. Change what it pursues or where its action lands, preserving its ability to hurt you.

**Game construction:** Bind a stronger charge to a visible lane; lure it into a wall whose collapse releases a burst against other enemies.

**Control and defeating observation:** Compare direct projectile/burst damage with matched damage and setup cost. Look for a distinct positioning or commitment decision.

**Boundary:** Enemy manipulation is a mechanism hypothesis; avoid attributing beliefs beyond the implemented state.

### RT08 — Turn residue into a resource

**Trigger:** Critique discards failures, misses and leftovers.

**Operation:** List what survives the failed operation: displacement, elapsed time, heat, exposed path, attention. Construct one bounded use and name disposal costs.

**Game construction:** A missed poison bolt leaves a temporary slowing line. It can redirect pursuers but also narrows the player's exit.

**Control and defeating observation:** Compare with an ordinary slowing cast at equal cost and lifetime. Reject a new-mechanism claim if the miss is merely an extra free cast.

**Boundary:** Attack-to-expand retains useful residue rather than treating failure as free reward.

### RT09 — Teach a pattern, then betray it

**Trigger:** The enemy is treated as either fixed or inexplicably adaptive.

**Operation:** Give the opponent a small visible memory. Let repetition build an expectation; make the player spend time teaching it before changing a meaningful dimension.

**Game construction:** Two successful clockwise escapes teach a guard to cover that exit. A counterclockwise escape creates one opening, then updates the memory.

**Control and defeating observation:** Compare fixed, bounded-memory and random opponents at matched strength. Measure exploitable preparation and explanation accuracy.

**Boundary:** Proposed finite state adaptation is not evidence of human learning or advanced AI.

### RT10 — Use an absent action

**Trigger:** The search assumes an additional attack is the only useful intervention.

**Operation:** List ongoing causal processes. Stop one deliberately and account for what decays, becomes detectable or transfers when it stops.

**Game construction:** Silence reduces detection by listeners while resonance charge decays. Holding fire preserves an unbroken poisoning route but sacrifices immediate damage.

**Control and defeating observation:** Compare against ordinary stealth/damage cooldowns. The test is whether choosing when to abstain changes the strategy.

**Boundary:** Indirection can be refusal, delay or withdrawal; doing nothing must have an observable state consequence.

### RT11 — Move rhetoric into a measurable input

**Trigger:** Expressiveness is being reduced to decorative dialogue.

**Operation:** Select an expressive act, map a narrow measurable feature to one game variable, and offer an equivalent alternative input before expanding its effects.

**Game construction:** Calibrated phrase pitch and timing generate bounded resonance; the phrase endpoint decides which gate receives it. A timing-button input supplies the same value.

**Control and defeating observation:** Compare voice, equivalent timing input and a plain damage multiplier. Test detection failures, fatigue, strategy and perceived agency separately.

**Boundary:** “Quality” must name what is scored. Musical beauty, identity and loudness are not inferred from pitch conformity.

### RT12 — Counter the assumption and rebuild

**Trigger:** The response to a powerful build is a blanket immunity or a larger health bar.

**Operation:** State why the build works. Change one enabling condition, retain some useful windows, reveal the change before commitment, and supply an affordable adjustment.

**Game construction:** A wall-breaker threatens one segment after a warning. Mason sacrifices it to reroute the charge and retains a second exit rather than losing all wall abilities.

**Control and defeating observation:** Compare blanket immunity, added enemy HP and assumption-specific counterpressure. Assess viable adjustments and fairness before claiming a curriculum.

**Boundary:** Defeating a strategy once does not prove creative growth. Later transfer needs its own evidence.

### RT13 — Separate literal, inferred and operative meaning

**Trigger:** An indirect sentence creates incompatible interpretations across roles.

**Operation:** Pass three fields: literal sentence; intended inference; executable condition/action. Require the next role to reconstruct each and preserve uncertainty.

**Game construction:** “Go ahead, hit me” becomes invite a named hostile direct hit while guard and stamina are intact; hazards and damage over time do not qualify.

**Control and defeating observation:** Compare unstructured rhetorical prompts with a matched literal contract and the three-field handoff. Score disagreement and lost obligations.

**Boundary:** This is an operational bridge inspired by pragmatics, not a theorem about language or creativity. AR04, AR06.

### RT14 — Distill after the attack, preserving the fracture

**Trigger:** Compression silently removes the awkward fact that made an idea possible.

**Operation:** The critic names the broken assumption and useful residue. Distill into trigger, action, cost, counter and falsifier; a fresh builder must reconstruct the changed dependency.

**Game construction:** Retain that the maze's exit also admits enemies. Builder adds a costly timed passage, rather than summarizing the idea as “make a better maze.”

**Control and defeating observation:** Compare full context, obligation-preserving distillation and a fluent short summary at the same downstream budget. Inspect reconstruction loss before judging novelty.

**Boundary:** Uses the existing Distill direction. The user's new word was contradictions; compression is only a supporting handoff technique.

## Reusable composition

1. Preserve a valued function without its familiar tool: keep the hero alive through three bosses.
2. State the incumbent dependency: survival assumes avoiding every contact, or routing assumes every enemy walks.
3. Apply one operator; retain its literal contradiction, cost and residue.
4. A builder constructs a rule or action; a critic supplies a concrete defeating case.
5. Distill the trigger, action, cost, counter and falsifier. Reconstruct them before advancing.
6. Compare against ordinary analysis and the equivalent literal operation with matched facts and budget.
7. Retain the candidate, classify it as an existing mechanism/presentation change, or record a no-opening result.

A sarcastic critique can be composed with RT14; temporal contradictions with RT07; prediction-led surprise with RT09; expressive input with RT10. Composition adds obligations: it earns no automatic credit from using more techniques.

## Prepared investigation

First cases: contact retaliation, a maze with a self-escape conflict, and a listener reacting to sound. Use three matched conditions: ordinary literal construction; operator with literal reconstruction; rhetoric only. Freeze each case's facts, build budget, output length and assessment rubric. Use source-blind assessors where feasible, but retain exact equivalence judgments and disagreements.

Primary outcomes: valid construction; actual operator application; preserved costs; mechanism family absent from the frozen comparator repertoire; feasible discriminating probe. Separately record idea count, readability, usefulness judgments and time. Do not label distinct words as distinct mechanisms.

For human learning, collect unaided delayed work on unseen cases after practice; compare with a control that gets equal domain information and time. Immediate assisted output establishes assistance at most. This protocol is prepared and has not been run.
