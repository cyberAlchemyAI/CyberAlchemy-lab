# Game precedents and novelty boundaries

Research date: 11 October 2026. Scope: a purposeful comparison of **17 existing games**, using official developer/publisher material and attributed creator testimony. GP01–GP16 investigate component and strategy overlap; GP17 adds a contrasting case of premise, experience and reception. These games were researched, not played in this session. This is not an exhaustive market search. Descriptions establish advertised features; omitted features remain unknown.

The confirmed concept is one deployed character chosen from three, a three-stage survival expedition with a boss in each stage, mid-stage acquisition, demanding manual execution or authored autoplay, and builds that encounter legible counterpressure.

## Where to look first

Inspect Minos for maze ownership and direct intervention, Gladiabots for behavior authoring, Transistor for planning/execution transitions, and Hades for build-dependent action windows. Then compare the two sound precedents, One Hand Clapping and Metal: Hellsinger. This is a focused study list selected by documented overlap, not a first-hand ranking of game quality.

Observe decisions rather than collect feature names: what information is revealed before commitment; what the player can author; when a plan can change; which costs persist; whether a counter forces adjustment or simply cancels an investment. A later hands-on comparison should record game version, mode and the actual inspected sequence.

Also inspect [Goat Simulator's contrasting case](/home/vlad_/projects/CyberAlchemy-lab/docs/proposals/hammer-bias-strategies/novelty/rhetoric-game-design/2026-10-11/GOAT-SIMULATOR-CASE.md). It expands the comparison beyond strategic decisions to expectations, humor, expressive outcomes and retellable experiences. The attached developer accounts document reported prototype interest; the proposed explanations for its appeal remain interpretations.

## Novelty ledger

| Candidate | Known ingredients / nearest comparators | Specific proposed addition | Current status and defeating comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authored behavior plus manual execution | Gladiabots, Transistor, Hades | One build exposes the same commands and resources to either a human or a bounded conditional policy over three boss stages. | Combination hypothesis. Compare against the identical build with fixed movement/skill priorities; if only convenience changes, do not call it added strategic agency. |
| Hazard avoidance plus contact retaliation | Survivor builds, action combat, conventional retaliation | Route cost treats terrain danger and enemy contact differently; the player deliberately accepts one risk to finance another resource. | Familiar effects arranged into a tradeoff. Compare against flat durability and automatic threat avoidance; test whether choices actually change. |
| Living inside an authored maze | Minos, Sanctum 2, Dungeon Warfare 2 | Walls route enemies and constrain the hero's own escape; policy must preserve exits through timed changes. | Strong precedent overlap. Maze construction itself is not the novelty claim. |
| Public enemy commitments | Tactical telegraphs, Baba Is You's semantic manipulation | Bind a scarce response to one identified attack and its stated success condition; success/failure changes where a resource goes. | Proposed mechanism. Remove token binding while matching costs and reward rates. Identical optimal decisions would defeat a structural-distinction claim. |
| Teach and betray an enemy model | Visible AI patterns and authored behavior | A bounded, visible memory of the player's repeated successful route can be deliberately trained and then exploited once. | Unverified novelty. Compare against a fixed pattern and an equally difficult random response; learning and fun are unmeasured. |
| Voice shapes topology and threat | One Hand Clapping, Metal: Hellsinger, NecroDancer | Accurate phrases reinforce a corridor; an imperfect phrase may misdirect a listener; silence suppresses detection while charge decays. | Combination hypothesis, not a first-ever voice mechanic. Compare against timing buttons and simple audio damage multipliers. |
| Counterpressure as curriculum | Difficulty progression and boss variants | Later encounters expose a declared assumption and provide affordable adaptation before the boss. | Design policy. Requires playtests for perceived fairness, transfer and retained strategic learning. |
| Unexpected premise and valued experience | Goat Simulator as a contrast case | Give existing strategic rules a surprising purpose and readable comic consequences; separate changed decisions from changed experience. | Experience hypothesis. Compare identical rules under different framing and repeat play after the initial reveal; interest may disappear with familiarity. |

“Not found in these descriptions” only establishes an unanswered search question. It does not establish absence from these games, other games, prototypes or published research. No proposed mechanic currently has an earned worldwide-originality claim or measured local gameplay advantage.

## Follow-up search and observation

For an addition that survives the small comparison, search by its operation: conditional behavior + action roguelite; intentionally taking damage + path authoring; enemy commitment + redirected reward; visible opponent memory + pattern betrayal; microphone pitch + combat + terrain. Include older games, prototypes, game jams and developer talks. Search synonyms and inspect implementations or actual gameplay where descriptions are insufficient.

Separate five verdicts: the mechanic can be implemented; players understand it; it changes a useful decision; the experience is perceived as surprising and valuable; the inspected precedent set does not contain an equivalent. A good result on one does not supply the others. A framing or experience claim can survive unchanged optimal strategy, but it needs its own audience comparison.
