# Ten strategies derived from principles of irony and sarcasm

**Date:** 11 October 2026. **IDs:** PR01–PR10.  
**Status:** Prepared, untested adaptations for general analysis and construction. These extend rather than replace the fourteen RT cards and twenty-four WM mechanic proposals.  
**Grounding:** [Principles, academic evidence and applicability](/home/vlad_/projects/CyberAlchemy-lab/docs/proposals/hammer-bias-strategies/novelty/rhetoric-game-design/2026-10-11/PRINCIPLES-AND-APPLICABILITY.md).

Each card names a principle, construction, domain example and comparison. An ironic or sarcastic phrase is optional. The strategy must produce an inspectable representation, alternative operation or experience proposal; a memorable objection alone is insufficient. IDs distinguish preparations, not proven novelty or global originality.

## PR01 — Attribute the expectation

**Principles:** P1, P2. **Trigger:** A claimed fact embeds someone's preference or prediction.

**Operation:** Write the source, expected outcome and conditions. Separate each from observed evidence. Identify a variable that could change the expectation's fit, then construct a conditional alternative.

**Example:** “Customers need more hammers” becomes “our sales process predicts customers will buy hammers when they need joints.” Propose a task-based intake that permits other joining mechanisms.

**Comparison:** An equally informed plain requirements review. Added value fails if it already makes the same distinction and construction, or if the attributed expectation cannot be evidenced.

## PR02 — Simulate the believer's commitments

**Principles:** P1, P3. **Trigger:** A familiar worldview hides its downstream consequences.

**Operation:** Briefly write from the perspective of a sincere believer in the claim. Trace which cases it would admit, reject or relabel. Stop the performance; reconstruct one consequence literally and build an alternative.

**Example:** An “all work is a ticket” organization omits coordination work that has no ticket owner. Construct a dependency handoff that gives that work a visible completion condition.

**Comparison:** A literal consequence trace with equal information. This borrows from pretense as a framing hypothesis; a performed critic is not evidence of authentic independent dissent.

## PR03 — Maintain a pair of interpretations

**Principles:** P2, P6. **Trigger:** An utterance, metric or artifact is understood differently by two parties.

**Operation:** Record its literal operation and each party's inferred meaning without choosing prematurely. Find a decision where the meanings diverge. Construct an observable distinction in the artifact or workflow.

**Example:** “Done” means code merged to the engineer and a usable feature to the customer. Add separate merged and verified-usable states with explicit evidence.

**Comparison:** A conventional ambiguity review. Reject the proposed addition if the states are already distinguished, or if users cannot understand the new distinction.

## PR04 — Translate the jab into a specification

**Principles:** P3, P7, P8. **Trigger:** Critique is memorable but does not tell anyone what to build.

**Operation:** Retain the informational objection; remove tone; identify the violated criterion and actual evidence. Turn the criterion into an acceptance check. Construct a candidate that satisfies it.

**Example:** “Wonderful dashboard: every chart asks me what happened” becomes “a user cannot identify the stalled step from the displayed information.” Propose status, cause and next-action fields.

**Comparison:** Equivalent literal critique followed by the same builder task. This strategy may show that sarcasm supplies no additional benefit; preservation of the useful objection is the point.

## PR05 — Abstract the relation and return to particulars

**Principles:** P4, P8. **Trigger:** A named tool dominates candidate generation.

**Operation:** Describe the required relationship one level above the tool; generate different realizations; return each to fixed constraints and a practical probe. Preserve at least one disqualifying condition per candidate.

**Example:** “Increase hammer force” becomes “establish the required connection under load.” Examine clamping, bonding or a removable fastener with material and maintenance requirements.

**Comparison:** Direct function-based reasoning. The sarcasm/abstraction literature motivates inquiry; this abstraction workflow itself is not a new established effect of sarcasm.

## PR06 — Partition an apparent contradiction

**Principles:** P5. **Trigger:** Two worthwhile demands appear mutually exclusive.

**Operation:** Test whether the conflict changes with time, space, actor, quantity or operating condition. Build a scoped arrangement; if incompatibility remains, quantify the tradeoff or identify an infeasible requirement.

**Example:** “Let users experiment, but keep releases predictable” becomes an exploratory workspace with an explicit, evidence-based release boundary.

**Comparison:** A plain constraint-resolution exercise. Two labels for the same behavior do not qualify; neither does asserting incompatible demands are simultaneously satisfied.

## PR07 — Inspect the success criterion

**Principles:** P2, P3, P8. **Trigger:** Success on a metric coincides with failure for its beneficiary.

**Operation:** Name what the metric actually counts, what stakeholders think it represents and where those differ. Construct a measurement or workflow that distinguishes the outcomes.

**Example:** Faster ticket closure coexists with repeat customer contacts. Link reopened issues to the original case and separately assess task restoration.

**Comparison:** A conventional metric audit. The method must permit finding that the current metric is useful for a limited purpose; ironic framing cannot prejudge its invalidity.

## PR08 — Build the world where the promise becomes true

**Principles:** P1, P5, P8. **Trigger:** A confident claim fails under current conditions but might have a feasible scope.

**Operation:** Ask which conditions would make the claim hold. Label each required change as available, fixed or unknown. Construct the smallest admissible arrangement and an observation that could refute it.

**Example:** “One interface for every user” becomes one shared information contract with role-specific presentation. Test whether the roles can complete their distinct tasks.

**Comparison:** Ordinary conditional redesign. A promise requiring impossible conditions is rejected; the method does not rescue every incumbent or prove the promise merely by stipulation.

## PR09 — Change the valued experience

**Principles:** P2, P3, P6. **Trigger:** The mechanics are familiar, but the purpose or tone could change.

**Operation:** Hold essential rules steady while changing the premise, actor or audience expectation. Predict the new experience and compare actual interpretations, enjoyment and repeat engagement.

**Example:** A public data exhibit presents administrative contradictions through an absurd “perfect efficiency machine.” Test whether visitors understand the underlying system rather than only remember the joke.

**Comparison:** The same information and interactions in a straightforward presentation. This extends the Goat Simulator lens beyond games; changed strategy is not required for an experience claim.

## PR10 — Distill the fracture into a constructive handoff

**Principles:** P6, P7, P8. **Trigger:** A critique must travel to someone who lacks the original conversation.

**Operation:** Save the attributed expectation, discrepancy evidence, useful residue, fixed obligations, permitted variable and construction question. Have a fresh reader reconstruct the meaning and build an alternative. Compare with the original context.

**Example:** An architecture objection becomes a short packet showing the dependency that fails during maintenance and asking for a serviceable arrangement under the same loads.

**Comparison:** A normal summary with matched length. Reject the handoff if it drops a decisive constraint, reconstructs an invented failure or yields the same repertoire with more effort.

## Composition with the existing techniques

Two lanes can keep evidence and construction distinguishable: the first records claims, conditions and observations; the second varies a permitted assumption and builds alternatives. Use PR04/PR10 to hand over a literal artifact, then PR05/PR06/PR08 to construct from it. PR09 evaluates a different outcome axis when the target is experience.

Distill can preserve the smallest usable discrepancy/obligation packet. A future Robot Talks investigation could examine a real tension across product, measurement, architecture or interaction layers when its applicability conditions are met. These are proposed compositions; neither skill was executed again for this principles update. Role names, simulated sarcasm and an agent's fluent response do not establish independence, human-like cognition or method efficacy.
