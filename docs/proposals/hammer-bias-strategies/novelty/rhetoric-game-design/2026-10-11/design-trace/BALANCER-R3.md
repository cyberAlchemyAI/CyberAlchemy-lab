## Stable tensions and minimal responses

### F1 — Novelty equivalence: the comparison class matters

**Claim:** “Same physical attack … can require different responses under different promised goals.” **Evidence/assumption:** the worked example establishes this within its model; broader distinctiveness is unknown. **Counterexample:** a conventional conditional reward keyed to the displayed objective can reproduce every token payoff and action ranking. **Minimal response:** claim a public-goal-dependent reward operator and a difference from the named goal-independent controls. Retain contradiction as a candidate expressive/strategic interpretation. Do not make an unrestricted claim that ordinary programmed effects cannot reproduce it.

### F2 — Unsupported knowledge: a scalar utility is not player preference

**Claim:** shield points and stamina receive illustrative values. **Evidence/assumption:** transparent assumed valuation, no measurement. **Counterexample:** low remaining stamina makes dodge prohibitively risky, or a shield expires before any future hit and has zero value. The illustrated ranking reverses or disappears. **Minimal response:** preserve the worked proof, and include these defeating states in the first decision probe. Measure whether goals affect choices without reporting the assumed weights as discovered preferences.

### F3 — Agency and cognitive load: expressibility is not usability

**Claim:** six composed rules and authored alternates support sacrifice plus recovery. **Evidence/assumption:** editor contracts are bounded; authoring comprehension remains unknown. **Counterexample:** a player writes a contact rule above a retreat exception and believes both apply; the first rule repeatedly prevents the intended sacrifice. **Minimal response:** use the promised rule-fired explanation in a paper authoring exercise before adding operators. Ask players to predict one selected action and repair one priority conflict. A failed exercise would justify editor repair, not automatic removal of authored tactics.
