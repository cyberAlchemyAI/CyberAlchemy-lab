# Twenty-four proposed mechanics: a portfolio for investigation

Date: 11 October 2026. **All mechanics are design proposals. None was implemented or playtested in this extension.** The initial slice selects WM01 plus the ordinary reflect/maze/magic systems; the wider set stays available for separate probes. Numbers are identifiers, not ranks or evidence grades.

Known ingredients overlap with the seventeen [game precedents](/home/vlad_/projects/CyberAlchemy-lab/docs/proposals/hammer-bias-strategies/novelty/rhetoric-game-design/2026-10-11/GAME-PRECEDENTS.md), including the added Goat Simulator contrast case. Distinct combinations are hypotheses. Each proposal names a state change, player cost/counter and defeating comparison; equivalence to an ordinary effect is an acceptable outcome.

This is a portfolio of outputs. The later [sixteen MG strategies](/home/vlad_/projects/CyberAlchemy-lab/docs/proposals/hammer-bias-strategies/novelty/rhetoric-game-design/2026-10-11/MECHANIC-GENERATION-STRATEGIES.md) provide methods for generating more mechanics; [four MC constructions](/home/vlad_/projects/CyberAlchemy-lab/docs/proposals/hammer-bias-strategies/novelty/rhetoric-game-design/2026-10-11/MECHANIC-GENERATION-EXAMPLES.md) show combinations worked into rule proposals. Some MG cards reuse WM seeds openly. These additions preserve the twenty-four WM entries below.

## WM01 — Invited wound

**Direction:** Contradiction / core candidate.

**Operation:** Bind a scarce response to a boss's announced touch, wound or arrival goal. Empower its attack; earn a capped shield/retaliation charge if that goal fails and the hero survives.

**Tradeoff and counter:** Blocking prevents a wound but still permits touch; interception can prevent arrival while hurting the hero. Spend one charge before the attack locks.

**Probe:** Change only the public objective in a matched setup. If responses remain identical, downgrade the mechanism-distinction claim.

## WM02 — The perfect charge

**Direction:** Sarcasm / enemy empowerment.

**Operation:** A selected taunt makes the next announced charge faster and locks its lane early. Failure to reach its named destination releases a bounded burst from the collision point.

**Tradeoff and counter:** The enemy becomes more dangerous; positioning a sacrificial wall costs time and wall capacity. A warned breaking variant can arrive through the wall.

**Probe:** Compare with an ordinary explosive wall at equal cost and burst. Test whether empowering and redirecting a particular commitment adds a decision.

## WM03 — A door that disagrees with time

**Direction:** Temporal contradiction.

**Operation:** Place a gate that admits the hero during a marked interval, then closes to pursuers; later it reopens as stored energy expires.

**Tradeoff and counter:** Crossing early leaves enemies a route; crossing late risks self-entrapment. A pursuer can pressure the reopening window.

**Probe:** Compare with a static choke and an ordinary timed trap. Keep only differences attributable to ownership of the crossing schedule.

## WM04 — Let the monster draw the spell

**Direction:** Indirection / movement as construction.

**Operation:** Place a short sequence of anchors. A pursuing enemy's actual path joins them; completing the figure releases magic at the final anchor.

**Tradeoff and counter:** The player must route a dangerous enemy through the shape, spending anchors and space. A shortcut, flyer or interrupted pursuit breaks the figure.

**Probe:** Compare with a pre-drawn damaging zone. Ask whether enemy routing becomes a necessary construction action.

## WM05 — Heal the boss into a problem

**Direction:** Irony / reversed benefit.

**Operation:** An acquired spell overgrows a boss's armor by healing it, extending a visible recovery while also increasing its remaining health.

**Tradeoff and counter:** The recovery buys an escape or poison setup; the player must later remove the additional HP. A cleansing phase sheds the growth.

**Probe:** Compare with an ordinary stun plus equal HP restoration. Do not credit originality if the choices are equivalent.

## WM06 — Protection broadcasts your position

**Direction:** Contradiction / information cost.

**Operation:** A strong magical shield emits a visible beacon that attracts particular enemies; switching it off suppresses the beacon and loses protection.

**Tradeoff and counter:** Use attraction to assemble a retaliation cluster or divert listeners from an exit. Ranged units can punish the broadcast.

**Probe:** Compare with separate shield and taunt abilities under a matched slot/resource budget. Test the coupled on/off tradeoff.

## WM07 — The maze is a battery

**Direction:** Topology / destructive-to-creative.

**Operation:** Walls store energy when enemies strike them. Destroying a charged segment releases slow or magic along the corridor it opens.

**Tradeoff and counter:** Release sacrifices routing and may open your escape to pursuit. Enemies can walk around walls rather than finance them.

**Probe:** Compare ordinary trap damage with the same geometry and resource ceiling; inspect the decision to surrender a useful wall.

## WM08 — Shed armor to build cover

**Direction:** Spatial contradiction.

**Operation:** Discard one temporary armor plate as a terrain segment, lowering reduction while creating cover or a funnel.

**Tradeoff and counter:** The plate is finite, destructible and occupies the hero's route. An unblockable lane or flanker punishes the armor loss.

**Probe:** Compare an equivalent armor debuff plus a normal barrier. The integration may be elegant without being structurally novel.

## WM09 — Poison the passage, clear your footsteps

**Direction:** Indirection / dual-purpose residue.

**Operation:** A poison field harms both sides. Moving through it with a limited cleansing skill carves a narrow temporary safe trail that enemies can also use.

**Tradeoff and counter:** The hero chooses between preserving poison coverage and buying a safe exit; a chase can use the cleared route.

**Probe:** Compare poison plus an ordinary safe corridor. Measure whether carving the path changes enemy routing and retreat choices.

## WM10 — Refuse the easy knockback

**Direction:** Contradiction / temptation.

**Operation:** Knockback moves threats onto a hazard but out of the current retaliation zone. Holding the enemy nearby maintains income and danger.

**Tradeoff and counter:** Reserve stamina for a later shove; a heavy enemy may consume the reserve first. Environmental kills produce no retaliation reward.

**Probe:** Compare with automatic knockback on every hit. Look for a meaningful decision to withhold an available skill.

## WM11 — Train the exit guard

**Direction:** Surprise / visible finite memory.

**Operation:** Repeated successful escapes on one side teach an enemy to cover that route. A visible two-event memory announces its prediction; change route to exploit one opening.

**Tradeoff and counter:** Teaching costs exposure and time. The enemy updates after the betrayal; repetition cannot create a permanent free opening.

**Probe:** Compare fixed and random responses at matched strength. Count exploitably authored expectations, not just unusual animation.

## WM12 — Pay interest on a missed attack

**Direction:** Residue / delayed construction.

**Operation:** A missed spell leaves a short-lived echo anchor instead of direct damage. A later hostile attack crossing it activates stored magic.

**Tradeoff and counter:** The miss spends the original cast cost and creates no immediate protection. The opponent can avoid or destroy the anchor.

**Probe:** Compare ordinary placed traps at equal power, lifetime and cost. Intentional misses need a reason beyond free extra effects.

## WM13 — An insult is a targeting promise

**Direction:** Sarcasm / indirection.

**Operation:** A preset taunt makes an enemy announce the last visible location of the speaker as its next target. Leaving a decoy there redirects the committed attack.

**Tradeoff and counter:** Taunting shortens the warning and consumes a decoy slot. An enemy variant telegraphs that it attacks the approach instead.

**Probe:** Compare normal aggro and decoys. Test whether the announced target creates a new risky planning opportunity.

## WM14 — Borrow a warning from the future

**Direction:** Information / surprise.

**Operation:** An item reveals one upcoming hazard category or boss objective before a choice, but reduces the current item's combat budget.

**Tradeoff and counter:** Use incomplete intelligence to pick an antidote, route exception or barrier. The revealed category does not reveal every spawn location.

**Probe:** Compare an equally costly raw-stat item and perfect foresight. Assess decisions made under partial advance information.

## WM15 — The slow route is the fast build

**Direction:** Contradiction / progression.

**Operation:** An XP modifier sacrifices current damage and requires visiting exposed cache locations; gains unlock a later branch before the final boss.

**Tradeoff and counter:** A safer early route can leave the branch unavailable. Rewards come from finite encounters; no respawn or damage-reflection farming.

**Probe:** Compare fixed progression and a numerical XP buff. Test whether acquisition geography changes the growth strategy.

## WM16 — Spend tomorrow's defense

**Direction:** Temporal tradeoff.

**Operation:** Borrow a shield for the current danger; its expiration creates a announced vulnerability interval that can be scheduled during a lull.

**Tradeoff and counter:** Borrowing during a boss combo can move the debt into the worst moment. A delayed enemy attack pressures repayment.

**Probe:** Compare an ordinary cooldown and fixed defense. Determine whether scheduling the debt adds an authored decision.

## WM17 — Countermelody turns a corridor

**Direction:** Voice / topology.

**Operation:** Matching a displayed phrase's relative pitch contour and timing charges a gate. Its final note chooses which corridor opens.

**Tradeoff and counter:** Resonance has a capped budget, cast commitment and audible attraction. The wrong endpoint opens an unhelpful route.

**Probe:** Compare equivalent timing input and a plain gate switch. Test whether phrases interact with strategy beyond input novelty.

## WM18 — Sing well, hurt harder, attract more

**Direction:** Voice / the user's damage example.

**Operation:** Calibrated pitch/rhythm conformity raises a capped magic burst and the radius at which listeners detect the hero.

**Tradeoff and counter:** High conformity increases both damage and exposure. Choosing a shorter or quieter game action can preserve stealth; microphone loudness itself gives no extra damage.

**Probe:** Compare voice with matched timing buttons and a plain quality-to-damage multiplier. Test whether the coupled cost produces choices.

## WM19 — An imperfect note is a decoy

**Direction:** Voice / reversed failure.

**Operation:** A phrase falling into a deliberately shown alternate pitch band creates a false sound source rather than the main damage pulse.

**Tradeoff and counter:** Detuning trades damage for routing, consumes the same phrase resource and can mislead the hero if its destination is poor.

**Probe:** Compare a conventional decoy with the same cost. Mistake conversion needs a controllable alternative, not punishment for noisy hardware.

## WM20 — Silence is a skill with decay

**Direction:** Absence / voice.

**Operation:** Stopping a phrase removes listener detection while accumulated resonance decays; restarting exposes the hero again.

**Tradeoff and counter:** Silence buys movement but spends stored power. A visible enemy pulse can force the player to choose between interruption and burst.

**Probe:** Compare ordinary stealth and charge decay. Full autoplay must author when to stop rather than always seek maximum score.

## WM21 — The enemy sings your last route

**Direction:** Voice / surprise / echo.

**Operation:** A boss repeats the last completed phrase as a sequence of marked hazard tiles. The player can deliberately feed it a phrase that leaves a planned escape.

**Tradeoff and counter:** Teaching consumes a phrase and a safe window. The copied path affects the hero too; a tempo change is announced before repetition.

**Probe:** Compare a fixed hazard pattern and a randomly selected one. Inspect whether the player constructs the next threat.

## WM22 — A chorus you must break

**Direction:** Voice / indirection.

**Operation:** Nearby enemies sustain separate tones. Removing or moving one changes a visible resonance field that can open a boss vulnerability or close a gate.

**Tradeoff and counter:** Killing the easiest singer may strengthen the remaining field; displacement can be better than damage. A replacement singer is warned.

**Probe:** Compare an ordinary kill-adds phase. The field needs a legible rule, limited combinations and an equivalent visual/timing representation.

## WM23 — Turn an execution into escape

**Direction:** Lethal terrain / enemy as actuator.

**Operation:** A lethal sweep is telegraphed with an escape route. An enemy's announced shove can carry the hero into a safe pocket if approached from the correct side.

**Tradeoff and counter:** The shove still damages the hero; failed positioning is dangerous. Issuance cannot depend on an unavoidable or undisclosed rescue.

**Probe:** Compare dodging the same sweep normally. Test whether accepting hostile displacement creates a feasible extra plan.

## WM24 — The boss shows you what it expects

**Direction:** Indirection / staged counterconstruction.

**Operation:** Before a stage, an enemy scout reports the observed feature it will pressure: staying still, walking a long maze or repeating one exit. The player can spend an item/route revision to prepare a counter.

**Tradeoff and counter:** Revising costs an alternative upgrade; the scout reports the bounded rule, not a hidden arbitrary build ban. Useful original windows remain.

**Probe:** Compare universal immunity and simple HP growth. Measure readable adaptation and later transfer separately.

## Sound and control-mode contract

For WM17–WM22, “quality” refers to calibrated relative pitch/rhythm conformity with a displayed phrase, not vocal beauty. The score is bounded; confidence failures are shown rather than treated as intentional musical choices. Loudness cannot increase damage. Calibration, environmental noise, latency and fatigue need a separate input study.

Manual mode may use live voice or equivalent timing/phrase controls. Full autoplay authors phrase selection, timing, interruption and resource reserves through the same action constraints; it need not fake singing errors. Maximum quality is not automatically best when it creates detection or chooses a bad endpoint. Compare modes separately; sharing possible effects does not guarantee equal difficulty.

Prepare local audio processing as the starting implementation assumption. Raw voice storage or remote processing is outside this design study. A visible microphone control should explain its use adjacent to activation. No audio was captured here.

## Portfolio discipline

Add one candidate at a time to a completed basic run. Require a changed choice and an explainable consequence before adding combinations. A mechanics count does not measure creativity. Preserve failed constructions and their useful residue so that a later builder can produce an alternative instead of reintroducing the same idea under a new name.
