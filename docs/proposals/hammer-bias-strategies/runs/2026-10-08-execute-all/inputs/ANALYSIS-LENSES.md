# Logical inversion and attack to expand

Status: local design extension requested by the user. These are proposed analytical methods, not established effectiveness findings or promoted Arcanum disciplines.

## Logical inversion

Invert an assumption about the intervention while preserving the stakeholder outcome and constraints. Ask which circumstances make the opposite intervention useful, which presumed necessity can be removed, or which causal direction is mistaken. The purpose is to expose a new degree of freedom.

Examples against the same review-delay problem:

- “More review automation reduces waiting” → “Under what conditions would fewer reviews reduce waiting while preserving required assurance?”
- “The requester waits for the reviewer” → “Could the empowered decision owner pull ready work rather than receive an unbounded queue?”
- “Every request needs approval” → “Which classes can proceed under an explicit rule, with exceptions reviewed?”

These reversals generate hypotheses. They do not establish logical equivalence or empirical truth. For a formal implication P → Q, its converse Q → P and inverse ¬P → ¬Q do not follow automatically. Classical contraposition ¬Q → ¬P preserves that implication, but an uncertain causal generalization must first be justified before treating it as a premise. Record whether the move is formal inference, counterfactual exploration, causal reversal, role reversal, or removal of a presumed necessity.

Smallest coherent unit: one assumption → one explicitly typed inversion → one candidate with a different mechanism → one discriminating test. Reject a reversal that changes the required outcome, violates a constraint, or merely changes words. Preserve an inversion that does not produce a feasible candidate as owned residue rather than manufacturing a solution.

## Attack to expand

Treat a critique as an opportunity to expand the design space. Attack an assumption, boundary, dependency, or failure behavior, then extract what becomes possible when that feature changes. Repairing the original idea is one possible result; replacing its mechanism or removing the need for it must remain available.

Use this conversion chain:

```text
attack or counterexample
  → exposed assumption or boundary
  → newly available degree of freedom
  → candidate mechanisms
  → smallest discriminating test
  → evidence-based retention, revision, or rejection
```

For example: “Automated summaries leave decisions unowned” exposes the assumption that information production creates decision capacity. It opens ownership, delegation, escalation rules, and removal of unnecessary approvals as candidate intervention families. Whether any solves the actual delay remains a question for evidence.

The attacker must identify a specific claim and show the counterexample or label the attack as an untested conjecture. The creative role converts a valid attack into an opening and up to three candidate mechanisms; at least one should differ from the incumbent where feasible. The synthesizer preserves the original outcome, identifies what was solved versus merely reframed, and records a test and owner. This does not mean accepting every attack or forcing an alternative when the critique establishes a hard constraint.

Possible terminal dispositions: invalid attack with reason; observed limitation plus testable expansion; conjectural limitation plus proposed test; hard constraint with no feasible opening; unresolved residue with owner. Actual dialectic runs still require sourced concrete counterexamples. The examples here remain hypothetical.

## Composition with the existing strategies

Logical inversion supplies candidate openings to Lane A. Lane Z continues to build and attack the incumbent. Attack-to-expand is a critique-to-creation contract across both lanes; it does not replace their independent responsibilities. Robot Talks can locate tensions, inversion can open different mechanisms, the creative role can elaborate them, and Distill can then select a small coherent candidate with a recomposition test.

Recommended ordering when expansion is the missing behavior:

```text
shared problem → evidence-backed tension → inversion/creative expansion
  → Distill → discriminating test → synthesis
```

Do not distill away alternative families before the expansion step. Conversely, do not reward option count: each retained candidate must connect to an exposed assumption, an unchanged outcome, and a feasible next test. Use up to three candidates per attack within the existing shared three-round route budget; do not multiply budgets through nested loops.

An expansion check can be activated on an existing critique step without requiring an additional full workflow. It passes when the attack's disposition, any opening, candidate fit, and next evidence are explicit. No empirical solution claim follows from a larger possibility space.
