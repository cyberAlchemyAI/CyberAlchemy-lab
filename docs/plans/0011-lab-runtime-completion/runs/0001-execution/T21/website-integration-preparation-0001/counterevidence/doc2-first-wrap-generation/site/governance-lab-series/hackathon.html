The challenge

How far can you take an idea?

Build a new solo project with Craft and CyberAlchemy Lab. Pick one ambitious behavior and let people try it.

Get to know Craft ↗Explore the lab ↗

Push it further.

Six directions. Your own idea is welcome.

01 / 06

Agent societies

Run a company of agents.

Give a small crew a real job, a budget, and conflicting priorities. Let them negotiate who does what. Then change the brief halfway through and see whether they can reorganize without you.

Use Craft to define roles and permissions; keep proposals, decisions, and results in the lab.

Try this Put the same crew through two different company structures. Which one gets the job done?

World building

Fork a city. Change its rules.

Start two cities with the same people, resources, and problems. Change one rule: who owns housing, how power is shared, or what happens when water runs short. Let the consequences play out.

Define the rules in Craft and preserve both histories in the lab, so people can trace where the worlds diverged.

Try this Give both cities the same crisis. Who thrives, who pays, and why?

Learning systems

Let a system rewrite its playbook.

Build a system that can notice a failure, propose a better rule, and put it to the test. Give it a way to reject its own fix when the evidence doesn’t hold up.

Represent each rule in Craft. Keep the failed attempt, proposed revision, and test results together in the lab.

Try this Hold back one unfamiliar case. Does the new rule help there, too?

Machine beliefs

Make a machine doubt itself.

Give a machine several plausible explanations for the same event. Let it ask for evidence, make a prediction, and commit to a view. Then show it something that doesn’t fit.

Model the competing explanations in Craft and trace their evidence in the lab. Make every change of mind inspectable.

Try this Mix a convincing false clue with an inconvenient true one. What would change its mind?

Tool invention

Teach an agent to invent its tools.

Give an agent a task its current tools can’t solve. Let it assemble a new instrument from small parts, test it in a sandbox, and decide whether it deserves a place in its toolkit.

Describe the tool’s contract in Craft. Record prototypes, failures, and successful runs in the lab.

Try this Hand the tool to a second agent facing a different problem. Is it useful beyond its first job?

Living art

Turn music into an ecosystem.

Make notes into creatures and rhythm into weather. Let a melody feed one species and threaten another. Give listeners a way to alter the music and watch the world respond.

Define the living rules in Craft and keep the world’s history in the lab, so a performance can be replayed or forked.

Try this Replay the same song with one changed rule. Does a different world grow?

Make one hard part of your big idea work.

The ground rules

One person.
One project. One vote.

Full rules · PDF ↗Word edition ↓
Build something new, on your own.

One new project per person, using both Craft and CyberAlchemy Lab. Teams and improvements to existing projects aren’t eligible. Libraries, frameworks, starter templates, and AI help are welcome; credit them and show your own contribution.

Your vote is yours, and it’s final.

Only registered participants on the final roster can vote. Pick exactly one eligible project other than your own. You can’t change a submitted ballot. Multiple accounts, automated votes, buying or trading votes are prohibited; organizers remove invalid ballots before counting.

The participants choose both winners.

The two eligible projects with the most valid votes win equal prizes. Think about originality, usefulness, what the prototype demonstrates, and how it uses Craft and the lab. Organizers check eligibility and counts; they don’t add judging scores.

If there’s a tie at the prize cutoff.

Organizers hold a recorded random draw among the tied projects for the remaining winning places. There’s no second voting round.

You keep your work.

Your original work stays yours. Public source code is encouraged, but optional; organizers must be able to verify the prototype. We may share your description, display name, screenshots, and demo with credit in the gallery and event promotion. Code ownership and licensing stay with you.

Be fair to each other.

Harassment, plagiarism, deliberate misrepresentation, or interference with a project or the vote can lead to disqualification. Organizers review concerns with supporting evidence and record their reasons.

What happens to your information?

Your account email is used to check eligibility and count one vote per person. Descriptions, display names, and demos appear in the gallery; emails and individual votes stay private. Identifiable registration and voting records are deleted 30 days after prize delivery and dispute resolution. Payment records may be kept separately; the public gallery and aggregate results may remain.
