## Following the evidence

A customer reports missing export rows. The agent finds a completion log and a screenshot with fewer rows than expected. Those records support different claims: what the job reported, and what someone saw under particular conditions. The Lab keeps the records, origins and supporting conditions so the assessment can distinguish them.

Provenance is the account of those origins and production steps. For the export, we need the request, inputs, software version, execution conditions, output, observation time and responsible recorder or transformer. A later reader should be able to distinguish an original observation from a summary, adaptation or generated view.

W3C PROV describes entities, activities and agents, giving us vocabulary for information, producing processes and responsible participants. The Lab separately records whether a source or revision has been accepted for a governing purpose. Knowing its origin helps assessment. Acceptance still needs its own record. [W3C PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)

Evidence work has stages. A Source identifies an origin and its content identity. Observation records research or experiment data with conditions and uncertainty; Operational Observation covers ordinary project work. A Measurement Method declares measurement, units, tools, sampling and uncertainty. A Measurement Result records an execution. An Evidence Set gathers support and contrary material for a question. A Finding states a conclusion within that support.

“The output contains 9,800 rows” needs an identifiable output and counting method. To say 200 required rows are missing, we also need an inclusion rule and identifier comparison. A claim that new pagination code caused the omission needs further causal evidence. Pagination retrieves data in successive batches. Saving the intermediate records lets someone inspect each step toward the conclusion.

Evidence supports a claim under stated conditions. One successful input leaves larger or changing datasets to be examined. Repeating the demonstration helps assess repeatability while preserving the original scope. The evidence needs the subject and version, claim boundary and conditions for continued use.

A digest identifies exact file bytes. A digital signature can bind an approval to a proposal and signing key under policy. Both help detect substitution. They leave truth, test sufficiency and the signer’s applicable permission to their own evidence and authority records.

This matters when importing CyberAlchemy v2 work. A faithful copy can still await Lab acceptance. An imported graph might omit current project objects. A source ledger records the transfer under declared rules, including renamed identities, missing links and older versions left unresolved. The Lab calls those meaningful leftovers residue.

Confidence needs a subject too. Evidence confidence concerns support for a claim; commitment confidence concerns an owner’s intention to proceed. A team can commit to an export redesign while reliability evidence is preliminary. Recording acceptance should preserve the uncertainty future experiments need to address.

A provenance view selects relevant sources and connections. It lets an agent follow the export through software, inputs, measurements, selection decisions and conclusions. The view needs coverage, exclusions and freshness, with unresolved references visible. Any conclusion still depends on the underlying records.

The assessment should say which comparison ran, what output was observed, which software revision applied and what is still uncertain about cause. It can name the owner for the next decision. Provenance makes the reasoning inspectable; evidence determines its support.
