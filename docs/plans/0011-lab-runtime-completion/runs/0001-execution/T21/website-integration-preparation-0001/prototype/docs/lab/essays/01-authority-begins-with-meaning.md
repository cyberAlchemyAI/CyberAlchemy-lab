## Authority begins with meaning

An AI sales agent prepares a renewal quote. The number looks plausible. Before anyone sends it, though, we need to understand what counts as a renewal, which pricing rule applies and who has permission to commit the company to the price. An approval button records a choice. The definitions and rules explain what that choice means.

CyberAlchemy Lab organizes project knowledge and work through identifiable records of definitions, plans, software, evidence and decisions. People and agents use those records to recover what applies to a task. A contract states requirements. Admission formally accepts an exact record or revision within scope. It supplies neither proof of truth nor evidence of deployment.

A Definition establishes meaning for a stated purpose. For the quote, “renewal” could mean extending an existing agreement rather than selling a new service. That distinction changes what the agent searches and checks. Its authority comes from acceptance of an exact version within the owner’s scope. A copied definition has a source trail; deciding whether that version governs in the Lab is a separate step.

Other records carry other responsibilities. An Artifact Type governs the required machine format and human views of a record. A Relation Kind governs a connection’s meaning, direction and permitted endpoint kinds. Policies state obligations or restrictions, and a Constitution combines rules for its subjects and scope. We need identifiable owners, versions and limits for each.

The agent might be able to calculate a discount while lacking permission to offer it. Assignment gives it a job; Delegation grants named actions within subject and time limits. A Principal is the identity recognized at an authorization or accountability boundary. A Role describes a position and expectations. None of the job, role or technical ability substitutes for permission.

NIST’s attribute based access control gives a useful comparison. It evaluates the requester, subject, operation and relevant conditions against rules. The Lab also needs the definitions and record requirements making that question meaningful. The comparison helps us reason about permission, without establishing that the Lab implements the NIST model. [NIST SP 800-162](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/162/upd2/final)

The same quote can be read through authority, provenance and lifecycle. We ask what governs and who is permitted to act, follow how the information was produced, then examine states and permitted changes. Other properties still need their own records.

The Governance-Axis Independence Invariant keeps kind, status, evidence confidence, commitment confidence, provenance and owner distinct where a decision needs them. An invariant is a condition the model must preserve. The Lab also tracks residue, meaningful unresolved or unpreserved work. An accepted pricing rule could still have limited evidence and unexamined customer segments, even when its owner is firmly committed.

A definition can affect later work through explicit links to a schema, validator, task selection or decision. Following that influence means identifying the applicable contract, implementation and observed effect. The definition itself executes no code and grants no blanket permission over related actions.

The inspected snapshot contains a tension worth keeping visible. The proposed governance module emphasizes decision rights, while accepted Authority and related definitions cover meaning and rules more broadly. Reading them together does not accept a revised module. That needs review and a decision. The sales workflow here is an illustration.

Now the sales agent has something to work from: an accepted meaning of renewal, quote requirements, pricing policy, permissions and required evidence. If something is missing, it can identify the gap and responsible owner. Another participant can later examine the same basis for the work.
