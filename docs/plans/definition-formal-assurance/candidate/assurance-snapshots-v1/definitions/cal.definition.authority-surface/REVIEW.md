## 4. Selected logical theory and implication directions

Readable notation for the three primary clauses is:

```text
∀s. Surface(s) ↔
     ProjectLocal(s) ∧ scope(s) ≠ EMPTY ∧
     (Allowed(s, NormativeMeaning, scope(s)) ∨
      Allowed(s, StructuralRule, scope(s)) ∨
      Allowed(s, PromotionDecision, scope(s)) ∨
      Allowed(s, Contract, scope(s)))

∀s. Surface(s) → CanCiteEvidence(s)

∀e,t. Evidence(e) ∧ BecameAuthority(e,t) → ResponsibleOwnerPromoted(e,t)
```

The four defining purposes form a disjunction. One selected purpose can suffice; all four may hold. The theory does not assert that all four must hold for every Surface, that each Source purpose has an accepted distinct denotation, or that no other permission can coexist.

`AllowedToDefine` means a selected modeled qualified permission. `DeclaredUse` and `RawDeclaredComplete` are different predicates: a declaration, label, path, shape-valid record, claimed permission, or complete set of fields cannot supply that qualification. Positive and negative models exercise this distinction directly.

`CanCiteEvidence` is capability, not an existential assertion of actual citation. Citation itself does not imply that evidence became authority in the citing Surface's scope. The third clause is a necessary condition; it is not a sufficiency rule, an actual promotion event, a definition of accepted evidence authority, or an adoption decision. Introducing the scope Token into this boundary reading is a candidate mapping. Real responsible-owner identity, evidence versions, context, time, causal order, and decision effects remain unresolved.
