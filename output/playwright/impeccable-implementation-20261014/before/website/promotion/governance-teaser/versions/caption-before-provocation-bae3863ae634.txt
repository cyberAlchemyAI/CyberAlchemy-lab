We’ve been working on a question behind CyberAlchemy Lab: what does an agent need to understand before acting?

Take an incident investigation. A customer says an export is incomplete. Before the agent proposes a fix, “complete” needs a precise meaning. The comparison needs a source trail someone else can inspect. The proposed repair needs a route through review and activation.

We organize those responsibilities through three lenses:

• Authority establishes which accepted meanings and rules apply, and who has permission to act within a scope.
• Provenance preserves where records came from and how they were produced.
• Lifecycle tracks each record’s state and the conditions for changing it.

An accepted definition gives the investigation a criterion. The assignment identifies the work. A current permission governs the agent’s allowed actions. Those records do different jobs.

When the session ends, the next agent should be able to recover the finding, its evidence, the open questions and the conditions for continuing. Long-term memory belongs to the project state. Short-term memory belongs to the session.

That’s the idea behind an open company brain: knowledge and work that people and agents can revisit, question and continue.
