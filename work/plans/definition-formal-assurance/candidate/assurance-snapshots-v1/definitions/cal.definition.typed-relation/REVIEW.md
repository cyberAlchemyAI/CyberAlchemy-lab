## Relation identity and ordered pairs

A connection has its own modeled identity. Its source-target-kind tuple does not become that identity. Two independently identified connections may share endpoints, kind and qualifiers. They may carry different evidence or qualifier records. This prevents accidental tuple deduplication from being presented as semantic identity equivalence.

An allowed forward pair does not permit its reverse. A separate explicit reverse contract can permit the reverse relation. A reflexive pair can also be allowed explicitly: source and target references then resolve to the same endpoint instance without the relation performing an identity merge. The source does not require every pair to contain distinct endpoints. Selecting `source != target` as a universal definition clause would overstate that source.
