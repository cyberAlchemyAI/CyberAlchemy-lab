## 3. Genus, orientation and intuition

Intent is the declared purpose, desired capability, problem pressure or change direction that orients work before the final output shape and implementation path are settled. The four orientation predicates form an inclusive disjunction. Purpose only, desired capability only, problem pressure only, change direction only and mixed orientation are supplied coverage witnesses. No mandatory Capability endpoint, immutable initial wording or predetermined artifact output is introduced. The plain-language intuition is what the work is trying to make possible before its final shape is known.

Selected records nominate attribution, routing bounds, revisability and traceability. These are necessary declared conditions in the candidate selection scope, rather than a sufficient real-world definition of valid intent. Actual attribution authenticity, route suitability, constraint enforcement and traceability remain independent targets. An Intent may still orient work after some output shape becomes settled; the model preserves settled and unsettled hypothesis cases without claiming a universal lifecycle policy. Source-owner review must assess those denotations and their relationship to discovery and routing practice.

## 7. Changed output and recorded why

The after record must have the desired hypothesis, and the change route must match the after record's nominated revision route. If the desired hypothesis differs from the original hypothesis, a declared reason must be linked to the change and recorded in that route. Desired state is separate from the after reference, so naming the original record as after cannot erase a real declared difference.
